China Hotel Industry Weekly: REITs, Renovations & Market Trends

China's hotel sector is entering a defining phase — major renovation investments are accelerating, the first Shenzhen-listed hotel REIT has cleared regulatory approval, and the country's top four hotel groups reported combined H1 revenue of approximately ¥29.8 billion. This weekly briefing distills the developments that matter most for international hotel developers, investors, and FF&E procurement professionals.

Our market intelligence team at Foshan FF&E tracks these signals weekly because they directly shape procurement pipelines, renovation specifications, and investment timing. Here is what the data shows.

Hotel Renovation & Expansion: A Multi-Billion Pipeline

The renovation wave across China's hospitality sector continues to gather momentum. Several high-profile projects reached key milestones this week, signaling sustained demand for FF&E specification and procurement services:

  • Hengyang Marriott / Huayi Hotel — Main structure complete, exterior glass curtain wall on track for October completion, targeting January 2027 trial operation. Features include a 36-meter pillarless banquet hall and helicopter pad, indicating upper-upscale positioning.
  • Zhangjiajie Hilton — Total renovation contract finalized with a ¥600 million investment across 273 guest rooms. Main structure completion expected by year-end 2026, with operations launching in H2 2027.
  • Liuzhou Hotel Brand Change — A landmark transaction: 13,446 m² rentable area on a 15-year lease, first-year rent at ¥6.21 million with 3% escalation every five years, and a 14-month rent-free renovation period. This structure is becoming a template for secondary-city hotel repositioning.
  • Shanghai North Bund InterContinental — Renovation budget approximately ¥45 million; Shanghai Poly Greenland Kaijia at approximately ¥31 million — both reflecting the Yangtze River Delta's focus on refined renovation of existing premium inventory.
  • Xiamen Shengyuan 5-Star Hotel — Total investment ¥1.525 billion, currently in sub-contracting stage. One of the largest single-asset hotel developments in the South China pipeline.

Additional active projects include the Zhangjiajie Wulingyuan Four Seasons / Yazhuo Hotel (¥50 million quality upgrade, construction started), Hangzhou Haihua Hotel renovation (over halfway through), and the Handan Marriott / Hilton dual-brand renovation (design bidding stage). For FF&E procurement teams, these projects represent significant near-term specification and ordering windows.

H1 Financial Results: Revenue Growth Driven by Cost Discipline

China's four largest hotel groups — Jinjiang, Huazhu, BTG Homeinns, and Atour — reported combined H1 revenue of approximately ¥29.8 billion with net profit around ¥4.4 billion. The consistent theme: profit growth is coming from cost reduction rather than top-line expansion.

  • Huazhu Group: H1 revenue ¥13.17 billion, net profit ¥2.4 billion. Q2 opened 498 new stores. RevPAR reached ¥238 with 79.8% occupancy. Notably, management franchise income exceeded 50% of total revenue for the first time — a structural shift toward asset-light growth.
  • Jinjiang Group: H1 revenue ¥6.801 billion, net profit up 47.05% year-over-year. 75% of central reservations are now corporate direct bookings, optimizing margin.
  • BTG Homeinns: H1 revenue ¥3.58 billion, net profit up 10.98%. Mid-to-high-end room proportion increased to 42.3%, with revenue contribution reaching 60.6% — confirming the upscale migration strategy.
  • Atour Group: H1 revenue ¥6.302 billion, net profit up 51%. Atour 3.6 and Jiye-format hotels achieved RevPAR exceeding ¥370 and ¥450 respectively. However, mature stores (open 18+ months) saw RevPAR decline 3% year-over-year — an industry-wide pattern signaling the shift from expansion to optimization.

The maturity-driven RevPAR decline across all major groups confirms that the industry has entered an inventory optimization and quality upgrade phase. For hotel owners, this means renovation and re-flagging investments are no longer optional — they are essential for maintaining competitive positioning.

Hotel REITs: A New Investment Vehicle Takes Shape

The Huatai Zijin Huazhu-Atour REIT received CSRC registration approval, becoming the Shenzhen Stock Exchange's first hotel-type commercial real estate REIT. Underlying assets include Guangzhou Tianhe Meiju/Atour and Shanghai Hongqiao Wanda Zi Room properties.

Meanwhile, China Gold Qiyuan Tourism REIT has advanced to feedback status. As of August 22, the total raised amount across all commercial real estate REIT applications on the market exceeds ¥75 billion.

This development is reshaping hotel investment logic. The traditional "years-to-payback" model is giving way to a focus on the ability to generate stable, securitized cash flow. Long-short mixed format properties are gaining regulatory approval preference. For hotel investors, the implication is clear: the focus is shifting from brand franchisee to asset holder, with emphasis on long-term asset value and active management capabilities.

Market Signals: Partnerships, Policy & Brand Upgrades

Several developments this week deserve attention from our international partners:

  • Marriott × Meituan Membership: Marriott and Meituan launched a joint membership program connecting travel and accommodation benefits. Meituan V4–V8 users can now claim Marriott membership — a significant distribution channel integration in the Chinese market.
  • Smart Hotel Policy: The Ministry of Commerce and seven departments are promoting smart home products, health monitoring devices, and smart products in hotels. Silver consumption has been formally included in the national consumption framework, creating policy tailwinds for technology-enabled renovation.
  • CLHC Established: The China Luxury Hotel Development Community was launched in September by Shanghai Marriott and Elitique Asia, aimed at promoting high-end hotel quality development — a signal that the luxury segment is organizing for coordinated standards.
  • Brand Upgrade Wave: International brands are upgrading to sub-brands (Sanya Marriott → Sheraton Grand; Chengdu/Suzhou Sheraton → Hilton) while local brands simultaneously upgrade (Shunde New World → Zi Room; Chengdu Yinhe Dynasty → Zi Room). This dual-track upgrading reflects both international and domestic competitive pressure.
  • Pricing Pressure: Shenyang Marriott's ¥12 buffet room service highlights the compression of high-star hotel F&B revenue and the overcapacity survival reality in certain regional markets.

Regional Market Hotspots

Northwest — Xi'an Leading Renovation Signings

Multiple established-brand city hotels in Xi'an have completed renovation contracts, including the Huacheng International Hotel signing with Yilong 2.0 and the Gujun Hao Hotel completing its overall renovation. The cultural tourism demand in this region continues to drive FF&E renewal cycles.

Central China — Localization & Quality Focus

Zhangjiajie Wulingyuan's Four Seasons/Yazhuo quality renovation is advancing, while Hubei Xiangyang's "city hotels + tourism" model is boosting consumption efficiency. All-season quality hotels in the region are reaching 90% occupancy rates.

South China — Landmark Transactions & New Supply

Guangdong Shaoguan Qujiang signed a seasonal hotel (100 rooms, expected opening before Spring Festival 2027). The Liuzhou hotel brand change has become a benchmark case for secondary-city repositioning. Xiamen Shengyuan's ¥1.525 billion 5-star development remains in sub-contracting.

Yangtze River Delta — Refined Renovation & Smart Upgrades

Core cities are focused on quality-driven renovation rather than new supply. Hangzhou Haihua Hotel, Shanghai Poly Greenland Kaijia, and the North Bund InterContinental renovations are all steadily advancing, with smart room technology and energy management integrated into renovation standards.

Shandong — Performing Arts Economy Driving Demand

Jinan's Phoenix Legend concert series is boosting surrounding accommodation demand, with walking-distance hotels seeing double-bed rooms sell out first. The performing arts economy is creating measurable short-term accommodation demand spikes across the region.

Franchise & Investment Landscape

As of year-end 2025, China's national chain hotel count reached 106,300 properties with a chain rate of 41.80%. Business hotels show the highest chain rate in the fine dining segment. Key developments:

  • Huazhu's 8-year 1,000-store breakthrough — with 300 million members and central booking penetration exceeding 65%, demonstrating the scale advantage of integrated technology platforms.
  • Jinjiang's multi-brand matrix covering economy to high-end, with mid-brand value index at 65.8 leading the segment.
  • Franchise economics benchmark: Using Vienna International as a reference point — brand authorization ¥300K, design ¥250K, deposit ¥100K, single room renovation approximately ¥110K, with a payback period of 3–5 years.
  • Tongcheng Travel launched a renovation empowerment plan for existing hotels, while Mid-Autumn/National Day advance bookings surged nearly 40% year-over-year.
  • GreenTree Alliance is focusing on sinking-market old store renovation with a light-touch upgrade strategy, targeting cost-efficient refreshes rather than full-scale reconstruction.
