Project Background

A Singapore-based property developer launched a new aparthotel brand targeting extended-stay business travelers and relocating families. The 150-unit complex featured three distinct unit types — studios (35m²), one-bedrooms (55m²), and two-bedrooms (85m²) — each requiring a different furniture configuration. The operator needed a single FF&E partner who could deliver kitchen + bedroom + living + bathroom as an integrated package, not just loose furniture.

Singapore's tropical climate — year-round 90%+ relative humidity — posed a critical material challenge. Standard furniture from temperate-climate factories develops mold within months if moisture content and wood treatment are not properly managed. The operator had experienced this issue with a previous supplier, resulting in a costly mid-contract replacement of 40 units. Our tropical-climate material engineering was a decisive factor in winning this project.

The project required phased delivery aligned with the building's floor-by-floor fitout schedule: studio units on floors 3–8 (first batch), one-bedroom units on floors 9–16 (second batch), and two-bedroom units on floors 17–22 (third batch). Each batch had to arrive within a 2-week installation window, with items palletized by unit type and floor level.

Challenges & Solutions

Challenge 1

Tropical Humidity — Mold Prevention

Singapore's year-round 90%+ humidity creates ideal conditions for mold growth on untreated wood surfaces. Previous supplier's furniture developed visible mold within 3 months, requiring costly replacement of 40 units.

Solution

All wood components treated with a two-stage process: (1) kiln-dried to 8–10% moisture content in controlled drying chambers, (2) anti-mold coating applied to all interior surfaces, edges, and back panels. Stainless steel hardware specified throughout to prevent corrosion. 12-month accelerated aging test (70°C, 95% RH) showed zero mold growth.

Challenge 2

3 Unit Types with Shared Production Lines

Producing 3 distinct furniture configurations (studio / 1BR / 2BR) from shared production lines without mix-ups — while maintaining the batch delivery schedule — required careful production planning.

Solution

Designed a 70% commonality platform: shared entry storage, bathroom vanity, and washing machine cabinet across all unit types. Unit-specific items (kitchen cabinets, bed frames, wardrobes) produced on dedicated line shifts. Each unit type color-coded (blue=studio, green=1BR, orange=2BR) from production through packing to container labeling.

Challenge 3

Phased Delivery — 3 Batches in 14 Weeks

The building's fitout schedule required 3 separate delivery windows: studios at Week 6, 1BR at Week 10, and 2BR at Week 14. Each batch had to be complete and defect-free for its installation window.

Solution

Staggered production schedule: studio items produced Weeks 1–5 and shipped in Batch 1 (2 containers). 1BR items produced Weeks 4–9, shipped in Batch 2 (2 containers). 2BR items produced Weeks 8–13, shipped in Batch 3 (1 container). Overlapping production windows maximized factory utilization while meeting all 3 delivery deadlines.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you prevent mold on furniture shipped to tropical climates?

All wood components are kiln-dried to 8–10% moisture content and treated with anti-mold coating on all surfaces including edges and back panels. Stainless steel hardware is specified to prevent corrosion. Accelerated aging tests at 70°C / 95% RH verify long-term performance.

Can you handle multiple unit types on a single apartment project?

Yes. The approach designs a shared component platform (typically 60–75% commonality) with type-specific variations. Color-coding from production through packing ensures zero mix-ups across unit types.

Can you deliver in phases aligned with our construction schedule?

Yes. Staggered production schedules allow batches to ship at intervals matching the fitout timeline. Overlapping production windows maximize factory utilization while meeting each delivery deadline.
