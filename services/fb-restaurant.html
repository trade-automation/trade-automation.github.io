Dining Chairs and Bar Seating

A 120-cover restaurant is 120 chairs, and the chair is where a restaurant package usually fails first. The failure is rarely the frame — it is the seat foam collapsing at month eight, the vinyl cracking at the piping, or the leg ferrules working loose from being dragged across a tiled floor twice a service. What follows is the seating range we build for restaurant floors, bars and lounge areas, with the construction that matters named under each piece.

Seats are high-density moulded foam over plywood or steel sub-frames, not the block foam used in residential dining chairs. Contract vinyl and woven fabrics are rated to 40,000–100,000 Martindale cycles depending on the tier, and fire compliance is to CAL 117 or BS 7177 for the destination market. Bar stools are supplied to your counter height with the footrest positioned off the actual counter, not a nominal figure. Send a floor plan with cover counts and the seating schedule comes back with quantities, layout options and a sample card.

Booth and Banquette Configurations

Fixed seating is where a restaurant layout earns or wastes its covers. A banquette run at the wrong depth forces guests to perch; a booth back 100mm too low kills the acoustic separation that made the room work on the drawing. These are configurations from our current restaurant programme — cane-backed booths on fluted bases, long banquette runs against slat screens, channel-tufted backs on brass plinths — each one adjustable in depth, back height, pitch and upholstery.

Booth carcasses are plywood and MDF over a steel or timber sub-frame, built as modules so a run can be delivered in lift-sized sections and joined on site without a visible seam. Seat depths run 450–600mm, backs 900–1,200mm, and the upholstery is contract-grade vinyl or woven with a foam density chosen for the seat rather than the back. Tables are matched to the booth pitch so the gap lands where your operations team wants it. Send a layout with cover counts and the banquette run comes back dimensioned, costed and drawn to the room.

Round Banquet Tables and Private Dining

The round table with a lazy susan is still the working unit of a hotel Chinese restaurant, a private dining room and a banquet hall. Everything about it gets specified: turntable diameter against the cover count, drum height against chair seat height, base material against how often the room gets re-set, and the edge profile against what a trolley will hit on the way in. Turntable diameter, cover count, drum height and base material are all specified per room rather than picked from a list. Motorised or manual turntables, fixed or knock-down drums, and the top in marble, timber veneer, lacquer or marquetry. Send the room dimensions and cover count and the table comes back drawn, costed and quoted against your chair height.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What furniture does a hotel restaurant need?

A: Hotel restaurant furniture includes: dining chairs, dining tables, booth seating, banquet chairs, bar stools, bar tables, host stand, buffet/server stations, wine cabinets, and outdoor terrace furniture. All restaurant furniture must meet commercial durability standards and fire safety regulations. foshanFFE.com provides complete F&B furniture packages including custom upholstered seating, solid wood or metal-frame chairs, and stone or solid surface tables.

Q: What are the fire safety requirements for hotel restaurant furniture?

A: Hotel restaurant furniture must meet fire retardancy standards: In the US — CAL TB 117-2013 (upholstery flammability) and NFPA 261 (upholstered seating). In the UK/Europe — BS 7176 (contract furniture) and BS 5852 (ignition sources). In the Middle East — BS 7177 medium hazard. foshanFFE.com provides fire-retardant foam, fabric treatments, and certification documentation for all restaurant furniture.

Q: How long does hotel restaurant and banquet furniture take to produce?

A: Upholstered dining chairs, banquettes and booth seating run 7-9 weeks because of frame fabrication and foam curing. Buffet and service-station casegoods run 6-8 weeks, and bespoke bar tops or feature joinery run 10-14 weeks. For a combined F&B scope the package is 10-14 weeks from purchase order to delivery before ocean transit, and ocean freight adds 18-30 days to Gulf ports or 30-40 days to the US East Coast.
