QC Inspection Points

  • Raw material inspection: Wood moisture content (8–12%), fabric weight/color fastness, foam density
  • In-process checks: Joint strength, edge banding quality, finish consistency
  • Pre-shipment inspection: Dimensional accuracy (±2mm), surface defects, function tests (drawer slides, hinges)
  • Packing verification: Corner protection, moisture barrier, container loading plan

7. Costly Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Mistake #1: Skipping the mock-up room
Saving $2,000 on a mock-up can cost $50,000+ in rework when 200 rooms arrive with the wrong finish color.
❌ Mistake #2: Choosing the cheapest factory
A $200/bed saving often means inferior joinery, thinner veneer, or untrained workers. Request factory audits and reference projects.
❌ Mistake #3: Not budgeting for compliance testing
Fire tests ($500–$2,000 per material) and formaldehyde tests ($200–$500) are non-negotiable for international projects. Factor them into your budget from day one.
❌ Mistake #4: Ordering all rooms in one shipment
Split into 2–3 shipments. First shipment = mock-up + first 30 rooms. This lets you catch issues early before the full production run ships.
❌ Mistake #5: Ignoring packaging specifications
Hotel furniture travels 10,000+ km. Specify corner protectors, foam wrapping, moisture barriers, and custom crating for fragile items (mirrors, stone tops).
❌ Mistake #6: Not planning for spare parts
Order 3–5% extra fabric, hardware, and replaceable components. Matching dye lots and finishes 2 years later is nearly impossible.
❌ Mistake #7: Underestimating lead times
Chinese New Year (Jan–Feb) shuts factories for 3–4 weeks. Summer (Jul–Aug) is peak season with 60–90 day lead times. Plan backward from your opening date.

8. Pre-Procurement Checklist

  • Complete design drawings (floor plans, elevations, FF&E schedule)
  • Material specifications and finish approvals
  • Fire safety requirements by destination country
  • Formaldehyde emission standards (CARB, E1, E0)
  • Electrical certification requirements (UL, CE, etc.)
  • Target budget per room (including shipping and duties)
  • Project timeline with buffer for CNY/peak season
  • Shipping terms decided (FOB / CIF / DDP)
  • Local customs broker identified
  • On-site installation team planned
  • Spare parts quantity calculated (3–5% extra)
  • Payment terms negotiated (typical: 30% deposit, 70% before shipment)

9. FAQ

What is the minimum order quantity (MOQ) for hotel FF&E from Foshan?

Most Foshan factories accept orders as small as 1 complete guestroom set for custom projects. For standard catalog items, MOQ can be as low as 10 pieces. For large projects (100+ rooms), dedicated production lines are assigned.

How much can I save by sourcing from Foshan vs. buying locally?

Typical savings are 30–50% on furniture and 20–40% on textiles/lighting compared to Western suppliers. Even after adding 15–25% for shipping, duties, and QC costs, net savings of 20–35% are common.

Can Foshan factories meet US CAL 117 and EU BS 7176 fire standards?

Yes. Reputable Foshan FF&E suppliers routinely produce furniture that passes CAL 117 (US), BS 7176 (UK/EU), and AS 1530.3 (Australia). Request test certificates from accredited labs before production.

What payment terms are standard?

The industry standard is 30% deposit upon order confirmation, 70% balance before shipment (after pre-shipment inspection approval). For first-time buyers, some suppliers accept L/C (Letter of Credit).

How do I handle US tariffs on Chinese furniture?

As of 2026, US tariffs on Chinese furniture range from 30–45%. Strategies include: (1) Factor tariffs into total landed cost — Foshan often remains competitive even with tariffs; (2) Explore bonded warehouse options; (3) For tariff-sensitive projects, discuss Vietnam production partnerships with your Foshan supplier.
