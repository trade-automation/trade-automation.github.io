Hotel FF&E Fire Compliance: CAL TB 117-2013 vs BS 7176 and BS 7177

Updated October 30, 2026  |  12 min read  |  By foshanFFE.com Editorial Team

Three fire standards decide most hotel upholstery specifications, and they do three different jobs. CAL TB 117-2013 covers the materials inside upholstered furniture sold in the United States. BS 7176 covers non-domestic seating in the UK. BS 7177 covers mattresses, mattress pads, divans and upholstered bed bases.

The pair that causes the most rework is BS 7176 and BS 7177. Both are British, both are titled around resistance to ignition, and both sort products into hazard categories, so they get treated as one standard for “furniture”. They are not. A seating specification that cites BS 7177 is citing the mattress standard, and a technical reviewer or a brand standards manager will catch it in the first pass.

A second assumption worth clearing early: Gulf projects do not run on British standards. Saudi Arabia works through SASO on the SABER platform. The UAE works through its Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice and Civil Defence approval. British standards appear in GCC specifications because a UK-based design consultant or an operator brand standard puts them there, which is common, but that makes them a contract requirement rather than a legal one.

What each standard actually covers

StandardCoversDoes not coverWho gives it force

Only the first is a federal regulation. The three British instruments are specifications written into a contract and given force by fire safety legislation, which is why they are negotiable at bid stage and not negotiable at handover.

Why the British pair gets swapped

BS 7176 and BS 7177 share a shape: four hazard levels, a cigarette test, a match test, then a wooden crib test that steps up with the category. The difference is the object and the test method. BS 7176 calls up the ignition sources in BS 5852 and tests a seating composite. BS 7177 calls up BS 6807, the mattress method, and tests a complete mattress or bed base.

Both also require that fillings meet the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988 at every hazard level. BS 7176 adds one more condition that catches suppliers out: every cover fabric has to go through a water-soak and line-dry procedure before testing, so a finish or a topical treatment that washes off will not survive the test.

Padded headboards sit in an awkward place between the two. A headboard with a cover and a filling is upholstered furniture, so the 1988 regulations apply to the filling. It is not seating, so BS 7176 does not classify it, and it is not a mattress, so BS 7177 does not either. In practice the operator's brand standard decides, and most of them call up BS 7176 Medium Hazard on the composite.

What CAL TB 117-2013 tests

The 2013 revision replaced the 1975 edition and changed direction. The 1975 standard carried open-flame tests on the finished item. The 2013 standard removed them and kept smouldering only, tested on small mock-ups rather than on furniture. The title says it plainly: Requirements, Test Procedure and Apparatus for Testing the Smolder Resistance of Materials Used in Upholstered Furniture.

SectionMaterial testedChar limitOther pass conditions
Section 1Cover fabric, wrapped over standard polyurethane foam, cigarette placed in the crevice between seat and back1.8 in (45 mm) verticalNo continued smouldering after 45 minutes; no transition to open flame
Section 2Barrier material, placed between a non-compliant cover and the foam2.0 in (51 mm)Same smouldering and open-flame conditions as Section 1
Section 3Resilient filling: polyurethane foam, polyester batting, natural fibre1.5 in (38 mm)Same smouldering and open-flame conditions as Section 1
Section 4Decking, the surface under a loose seat cushion1.5 in (38 mm)Same smouldering and open-flame conditions as Section 1

Char limits and the 45-minute observation window come from the standard. Three mock-ups are tested per material; one failure triggers a further three, and all of those must pass.

Three consequences follow for a bill of materials. If the cover fabric passes Section 1, the first filling layer below it still has to pass Section 3. If the cover fabric fails Section 1, it can still be used behind a barrier that passes Section 2, and the barrier has to cover the top and all sides of the cushion. If a filling fails Section 3, a passing barrier between the cover and that filling brings it back into scope.

The standard states in its own text that the methods are not intended to measure performance under open-flame exposure, and that mock-up results do not necessarily predict the same material in a full-size piece. A specification that calls for open-flame performance on a US hotel project therefore needs its own report, usually CAL TB 133 for high-risk public occupancy, and a CAL TB 117-2013 report cannot stand in for it.

Since 25 June 2021 the standard is federal under 16 CFR 1640, not a California rule that other states follow by habit. Each item carries a permanent label reading “Complies with U.S. CPSC requirements for upholstered furniture flammability”, and that label is itself the certification. The label obligation began on 25 June 2022.

What BS 7176 requires of contract seating

The hazard category is set by the fire risk assessment, not by the supplier. The table below follows the end-use examples published with the standard, and the pattern is worth reading carefully, because it is the opposite of what most people assume: a hotel restaurant is Medium Hazard, not High.

Hazard levelWhat has to passTypical premises
Low EN 1021-1 smouldering cigarette, EN 1021-2 match flame. No crib. Offices, schools, colleges, universities, museums, exhibitions, day centres
Medium Low, plus BS 5852 ignition source 5, the Crib 5 wooden crib, applied to the cover and filling composite Hotel bedrooms, restaurants, bars, public buildings, hospitals, casinos, public halls, places of entertainment, student accommodation
High Low, plus ignition source 7, a larger crib Sleeping accommodation in certain hospital wards, certain hostels, offshore installations
Very high Low, plus any ignition source from BS 5852 clause 11 or 12, at the specifier's discretion Prison cells, locked psychiatric accommodation

A hotel guestroom and a hotel restaurant both sit at Medium Hazard. High Hazard belongs to settings where occupants may be unable to self-evacuate, not to busy public areas.

Three requirements sit underneath every category and are easy to miss. Fillings must meet the 1988 regulations at all four levels. Cover fabrics must be water-soaked and line-dried before testing. And the standard sets a retest interval: each cover and filling combination is retested every 2,500 units or every 20,000 metres of fabric, or once a month, whichever is more practical, and again on any change of specification or supplier. A colour change on its own does not trigger a retest.

One term needs untangling. The “5” in Crib 5 is the ignition source number, not a count of timber layers. Source 5 is a wooden crib roughly 65 mm tall, lit with a small amount of alcohol, standing in for a burning wastepaper bin. Source 7 is a larger crib of the same construction. Because a Crib 7 pass depends on the whole build-up, the cover, filling and any interliner have to be qualified together.

What BS 7177 requires of mattresses and bed bases

BS 7177 uses the same four hazard levels but a different test method, BS 6807, and a different set of end uses. For a hotel project the relevant category is almost always Medium Hazard, and the deliverable is a label as much as a report.

Hazard levelWhat has to passTypical premises
Low EN 597-1 smouldering cigarette, EN 597-2 match flame Domestic dwellings, including non-motorised caravans
Medium Low, plus BS 6807 source 5 Hotels, hospitals, hostels, boarding schools, halls of residence, holiday camp chalets, care homes, residential schools
High Low, plus BS 6807 source 7 Certain hospital wards, certain hostels, hotels, offshore installations, care homes
Very high Low, plus source 7 and any further requirement the specifier sets Locked psychiatric accommodation, prison cells

Test method is BS 6807, not BS 5852. A supplier who offers a BS 5852 Crib 5 report against a BS 7177 requirement has tested to the wrong method.

The label is prescribed. The word RESISTANT appears on the border in white at a minimum letter height of 5 mm, with a smouldering cigarette symbol and a flaming match symbol in black on the central white area, and the wording “Conforms to BS 7177 for medium hazard” or whichever level applies. A mattress that passes testing but ships without the correct label is not compliant at handover.

The definition of a mattress in the standard is wide enough to include mattress pads and mattress toppers, so a topper ordered separately for a guestroom is in scope. Fillings must meet the 1988 regulations at every level here as well.

What GCC projects actually require

British standards do not have legal standing in the Gulf. The legal instruments are national, and they are administered differently from country to country.

MarketLegal instrumentHow conformity is evidenced
Saudi ArabiaSASO technical regulations for furniture and interior productsProduct Certificate through SABER before shipment, then a Shipment Certificate per consignment
UAEUAE Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice, administered with Civil DefenceDesign submission by the consultant, then Civil Defence inspection and approval of installed FF&E
Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, BahrainNational civil defence codes, largely modelled on NFPA and on the UAE codeProject-specific; the consultant confirms the route at design stage

Where a GCC specification names BS 7176, it is because an operator brand standard or a UK-based consultant put it there. That makes it a contractual obligation with a named test, which is enforceable against the supplier in a way that a code reference often is not.

This matters at bid stage. A supplier who reads “BS 7176 Medium Hazard” in a Dubai specification and quotes a Crib 5 composite test has priced the right work. A supplier who reads “BS 7177” there and quotes a mattress test has priced the wrong work, and the error surfaces when the fabric library is submitted.

Mistakes that show up in submittals

MistakeWhy it happensWhat it costs
Citing BS 7177 for seating Both British standards carry the words resistance to ignition and both use four hazard categories, so they read as one standard for furniture Rejected at technical review. Retesting to BS 7176 adds two to three weeks and may narrow the fabric library
Specifying High Hazard for a hotel restaurant or lobby High sounds safer, and nobody wants to be the person who under-specified a fire requirement Crib 7 testing is a heavier test on a smaller pool of qualified fabrics. The correct category is Medium, and the over-specification is paid for twice
A Crib 5 certificate with no cigarette or match stage Suppliers quote the headline test because it is the one the buyer recognises BS 7176 Medium requires all three tests. A Crib 5-only report does not close the category, whatever the certificate says
A CAL TB 117-2013 report on the fabric alone Section 1 is the section everyone knows, and fabric mills supply it readily The filling below the cover still has to pass Section 3. A fabric-only report leaves half the bill of materials uncovered
Treating a test report as a certificate The words get used interchangeably in quotation packs A report names a specimen. A certificate names the item and the hazard category. The fire officer asks for the second
Assuming an EN 1021 pass covers a UK contract EN 1021-1 and -2 are the visible European baseline and appear on most fabric datasheets EN 1021 is the Low Hazard level. Medium adds Crib 5 on the composite, so the datasheet line does not reach the requirement

Five of these six are specification errors rather than manufacturing errors, which means they are cheap to fix before the order and expensive to fix after it.

Name the standard, the edition and the hazard category. A line that says “fire retardant upholstery” gives the supplier nothing to test against and gives the reviewer nothing to check.

What the supplier has to hold

Compliance is decided when the bill of materials is fixed, not when the chair is finished. For a hotel package the working sequence runs like this:

  • Specification review. Confirm which instruments are legally mandatory at the destination, which are brand requirements, and which hazard category applies to each space, before pricing.
  • Material selection from a tested library. An integrator holding pre-tested cover, foam and barrier combinations can quote without waiting four to six weeks for new testing. Ask for the library and the report numbers behind it.
  • Composite testing before production. For a large package, confirm the specified combination passes as a composite. A fabric that passes alone can fail against a different foam density.
  • Locked substitutions. Any change of foam grade, barrier, backing fabric or even thread count requires revalidation. This is the most common route by which a compliant sample becomes a non-compliant shipment.
  • Documentation package. Reports, certificates and batch records travel with the goods. The label is part of the deliverable, not an afterthought at packing.

For a buyer, the useful question at supplier selection is not whether the factory has certificates, but whether it can name the report number behind a specific cover-and-foam combination and show the retest date. A certificate with no report behind it is a claim. A report with a named specimen and a date is evidence.

Frequently asked questions

Is BS 7177 the right standard for hotel chairs?

No. BS 7177 covers mattresses, mattress pads, divans and upholstered bed bases. Non-domestic seating, including hotel chairs and sofas, is BS 7176. If a specification asks for BS 7177 on seating, it has named the wrong standard and the requirement needs clarifying before anyone tests to it.

Is CAL TB 117-2013 still a California-only requirement?

No. Since 25 June 2021 it is the federal flammability standard for upholstered furniture under 16 CFR 1640, enforced by CPSC, and it applies to furniture manufactured, imported or reupholstered on or after that date. The permanent label requirement started on 25 June 2022.

Does a Crib 5 certificate mean the furniture will not burn?

No. Crib 5 is a resistance test, not a fireproofing claim. It means the composite did not ignite progressively when a small wooden crib burned against it. The furniture will still burn in a developed fire. What the rating buys is the time occupants need to leave.

Which hazard category applies to a UK hotel guestroom?

Medium, for both seating under BS 7176 and mattresses under BS 7177. High Hazard belongs to settings where occupants may not be able to self-evacuate, such as certain hospital wards and offshore installations, not to guestrooms or restaurants.

Do GCC projects require British standards?

Not as law. The legal requirements are SASO in Saudi Arabia and the Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice with Civil Defence approval in the UAE. British standards appear when an operator brand standard or a UK-based consultant writes them into the performance specification, which is common and which makes them contractually binding even though they are not statutory.

Can one test report cover a whole seating package?

No. Fire performance is attached to a specific cover-and-filling combination. Change the fabric, the foam or the barrier and the report no longer describes the product. A package with twelve upholstered items in four fabric and two foam combinations needs evidence for each combination, not one report per order.

The short version. CAL TB 117-2013 is a federal US regulation on components, tested by smouldering. BS 7176 is the UK specification for non-domestic seating, with Medium Hazard as the hotel default. BS 7177 is the UK specification for mattresses and bed bases. Nothing in the Gulf is legally British, and a specification that swaps the two British standards will not survive technical review.
