Hotel Furniture Board Materials: How to Choose, Specify and Machine Each One

foshanFFE.com · October 30, 2026 · 15 min read

A hotel furniture specification that names only “plywood” or “MDF” has not specified a board. Both are families, not products. EN 312 alone defines seven particleboard grades from P1 to P7, and two boards that both read P2 can differ by 100 kg/m3 in density and by half in screw withdrawal strength.

The board choice drives three things a hotel notices inside the first year: whether a wardrobe shelf sags, whether a bathroom vanity swells at a cut edge, and whether a door face blisters. None of those failures come from the wrong brand. They come from the wrong grade in the wrong place, machined the wrong way.

This guide covers the five board families that account for almost all hotel casegoods and millwork — plywood, MDF and HDF, particleboard, high-pressure laminate, and moisture-resistant board. For each one it sets out the European grade system that defines it, the mechanical values worth writing into a purchase order, and the machining rules that decide whether it survives installation.

One note before the detail. In Chinese factory usage the term 防火板 almost always means high-pressure laminate, the decorative surface, not a fire-rated substrate. Those are different products under different standards, and treating them as one is among the more expensive mistakes available on a hotel project. Both are covered below.

Density ranges are typical commercial values at 18 mm, not minimums set by the standards. Where a grade sets a mechanical requirement, it is the internal bond and bending strength that get tested, not the density.

Plywood: the only board here that carries structure

Plywood is cross-laminated veneer, and the alternating grain direction is what makes it the only board in this set that can span, cantilever and hold a fixing under load. That property comes from the glue line, which is why the bond class matters more than the thickness on the label.

EN 636 sets three service conditions, and EN 314-2 sets three bond classes that map onto them. A board sold as “plywood” with no service class is a board with no declared bond performance, which in a bathroom is a guess.

EN 636 partService conditionBond class requiredHotel useEvidence to ask for
EN 636-1Dry interiorEN 314-2 Class 1Guestroom carcasses, wardrobe internalsBond class stated in the panel marking
EN 636-2Humid interiorEN 314-2 Class 2 or 3Vanity carcasses, wall-hung unitsEN 314-2 test report for the batch
EN 636-3ExteriorEN 314-2 Class 3Balcony, terrace and poolside millworkEN 314-2 Class 3 plus the EN 636-3 declaration

Class 3 glue is the only one rated for repeated wetting. A bathroom vanity built from EN 636-1 board with Class 1 glue is a dry-interior product installed in a humid room, and the failure will appear at the back edge first.

Two checks separate real plywood from a look-alike. Count the plies: an 18 mm board should show about thirteen, a 12 mm board about nine, and a board with far fewer is blockboard or lumber core dressed as plywood. Then look at the face. Patched and plugged faces are normal in a lower face grade, but a veneer under 0.5 mm laid over a patched face will show every repair as a shadow once finished.

Machining follows from the structure. Plywood holds screws better than particleboard in both face and edge, takes a clean rebate, and can be worked without edge banding on concealed parts. It also moves less than solid wood in width, which is why it suits long vanity tops and wall-hung units where a 1,800 mm span has to stay flat.

MDF and HDF: the substrate that takes veneer and paint

MDF is the default substrate for anything veneered or painted, because its fibre structure has no grain direction and no voids, so a machined edge stays crisp and a veneer face has nothing underneath to telegraph. EN 622-5 defines six types, and only two of them belong in a hotel.

EN 622-5 typeService conditionLoad-bearing24h swelling at 18 mmInternal bond at 18 mmHotel use
MDFDryNo12% max0.55 N/mm2Guestroom casegoods, veneered and painted faces
MDF.HHumidNo8% max0.75 N/mm2Bathroom vanities, spa, poolside joinery
MDF.LADryYes12% max0.55 N/mm2Load-bearing shelves, bench seats, seat platforms
MDF.HLSHumidYes8% max0.75 N/mm2Load-bearing humid fitments

Figures are the EN 622-5 requirements for the 12 to 19 mm thickness band. Two further types exist, L-MDF and UL-MDF, defined for lightweight panels at lower density; both are dry-condition products and neither belongs in a load-bearing hotel application.

HDF sits outside this system. EN 316 defines high-density fibreboard by density at 800 kg/m3 and above, but EN 622-5 — the part that sets performance requirements — has no HDF type. There is therefore no harmonised class to test an HDF board against, and an HDF claim rests entirely on the mill data sheet. That is not a reason to avoid the material; it is a reason to attach the data sheet to the order and test against it on arrival.

What HDF buys is an edge. At 800 kg/m3 and above, a routed profile holds its shape, a painted edge sands without fuzzing, and a hinge screw does not tear out after the fourth adjustment. Door faces and any component with a machined edge are where the money is well spent. Structural spans are where it is not, because no EN class covers it.

Machining MDF punishes two habits. Screws driven without a pilot hole will swell the surface and leave a raised ring that no finish will hide. And because MDF has no grain, a screw close to an edge splits the board along whatever line the stress finds, so edge distance matters more than it does in solid timber.

Particleboard: the cheapest board and the one most often misapplied

Particleboard is the workhorse of hotel casegoods and the board most often specified by price rather than grade. EN 312 defines seven types, and the distinction between them is not cosmetic: it is the difference between a carcass that lasts the refurbishment cycle and one that swells at the plinth within a season.

EN 312 typeService conditionLoad-bearingHotel useReject if
P1Dry, general purposeNoNot a hotel furniture boardSpecified for any furniture or fitment
P2Dry interior fitments including furnitureNoWardrobe sides, shelves, backs, drawer boxesSpecified for a bathroom, kitchen or utility room
P3Humid, non-load-bearingNoHumid interior fitments without loadUsed where it carries load
P4Dry, load-bearingYesLoad-bearing shelves and bench seatsUsed in a humid room
P5Humid, load-bearingYesBathroom carcasses, utility fitmentsUsed in standing water
P6Dry, heavy-duty load-bearingYesHeavy-duty shelving, high-load platformsUsed in a humid room
P7Humid, heavy-duty load-bearingYesHeavy-duty humid fitmentsUsed in standing water

P2 and P5 are the two grades a hotel project will actually use. P3, P4, P6 and P7 exist and are occasionally specified on structural work, but they are rare in furniture and their presence in a quotation usually signals a copy-paste from a construction spec.

The P2 and P5 difference is measurable. P2 is bound with urea-formaldehyde resin and rated for dry interior conditions. P5 uses melamine-urea-formaldehyde, carries a green-dyed core, and is rated for humid conditions while carrying load. On an 18 mm board, internal bond rises from 0.35 to 0.45 N/mm2 and bending strength from 11 to 16 N/mm2. Density moves from around 630 to between 650 and 720 kg/m3. The price difference is typically 15 to 25 percent, against the cost of replacing a swollen vanity carcass after handover.

Particleboard's weak point is its edge. The core is a mat of chips with lower density near the faces, so a screw driven into the edge holds far less than one driven into the face, and a cut edge left unsealed absorbs water faster than any other board here. Three rules follow: keep edge fixings at least 50 mm from the corner, band all four edges on any panel a guest can see, and never rout a groove deeper than a third of the board thickness.

防火板: the term covers two different products

Chinese-language specifications use 防火板 for two unrelated things. The first is high-pressure laminate, HPL — the decorative phenolic-faced sheet bonded to a core, used on worktops, bar tops and door faces. It carries the name because phenolic resin is slow to ignite, not because the sheet is a fire-rated product. The second is a genuinely fire-retardant substrate, a board treated or manufactured to reach a Euroclass B or C rating.

A specification that says 防火板 without saying which of the two is being ordered cannot be priced, cannot be inspected and cannot be accepted. They are ordered from different suppliers and tested to different standards.

High-pressure laminate, EN 438

HPL is classified by how it will be used and whether it carries a flame-retardant formulation. EN 438-3 covers laminates under 2 mm thick that are bonded to a substrate, and it holds both horizontal and vertical grades. EN 438-4 covers compact laminate 2 mm and thicker, which carries its own load. EN 438-9 covers alternative core constructions.

HPL gradeStandardNominal thicknessWhere it belongsDo not use
HGS, horizontal general purposeEN 438-30.8 to 1.0 mmGuestroom desk and table tops, vanity topsVertical faces, where it is over-specified
HGF, horizontal flame retardantEN 438-30.8 to 1.0 mmPublic area bar tops, buffet countersAnywhere a standard grade already meets the brief
VGS, vertical general purposeEN 438-30.6 to 0.8 mmDoor faces, wardrobe fronts, wall panelsHorizontal surfaces under load
VGF, vertical flame retardantEN 438-30.6 to 0.8 mmCorridor and escape route wall liningsHorizontal surfaces
CGS, compact interiorEN 438-42 mm and aboveDoor faces and worktops where an exposed edge is wantedCurved work; compact does not postform

The F in a grade code means the sheet has a flame-retardant formulation and carries a Euroclass declaration. The S means standard. A quotation that names HPL without naming the grade is quoting a price for an unspecified product.

Four numbers decide whether a laminate survives a hotel. Density at 1.35 g/cm3 or above. Boiling water resistance at rating 5 under EN 438-2, clause 12, which means no visible change after immersion. Resistance to a 20 N small-diameter ball impact. And dimensional stability in the sheet, so a 2 mm laminate does not grow past its substrate at the corners of a bar top. Below those figures the surface will craze at the edge within a season, and no amount of care in the factory will prevent it.

One limitation gets missed. HPL is not resistant to continuous moisture exposure. EN 438-3 states that panels must be able to dry continuously; where water sits on an edge or behind an unsealed back, the phenolic core blisters and the face lifts. A hotel bathroom counter needs a sealed substrate and sealed edges, not merely a laminate face.

Fire-retardant board, EN 13501-1

A fire-retardant board is a substrate, and it is classified by its reaction to fire rather than by any surface. EN 13501-1 gives the Euroclass, followed by two suffixes: smoke production from s1 to s3, and flaming droplets from d0 to d2.

EuroclassWhat it meansBoard that reaches itHotel location
A2-s1,d0Non-combustible, negligible smoke, no dropletsMineral core board, cement particle boardService shafts, kitchen extract, escape stairs
B-s1,d0Very limited contribution, low smoke, no dropletsFully treated FR MDF or FR particleboardCorridors, escape routes, public area wall linings
C-s1,d0Limited contribution, low smoke, no dropletsFR board at a lighter treatment levelGuestroom wall linings, lobby feature panels
D-s2,d0Acceptable contribution, medium smokeUntreated MDF or particleboard, density 600 kg/m3 or more, 9 mm and above, mounted with no air gapGuestroom casegoods and general furniture
EBasic reaction to fireUntreated board below those density and thickness thresholds, or mounted over an air gapNot for hotel furniture

Untreated particleboard and MDF reach D-s2,d0 at a density of 600 kg/m3 or more and a thickness of 9 mm or more, provided the panel is mounted with no air gap behind it. The same board over a ventilated cavity drops to D-s2,d2, and a thin or low-density board falls to E. The variable that decides the class is the fixing detail as much as the board, which is why a Euroclass certificate quotes the mounting condition. Most hotel casegoods therefore need no treatment at all; the requirement appears in corridors, escape routes and public areas, where B-s1,d0 or C-s1,d0 is commonly written.

Two practical points. First, ask for the classification report rather than the word “fireproof”, because the report names the board, the thickness and the treatment, and a generic claim names nothing. Second, treatment changes the board: FR MDF is denser, machines differently and costs materially more, so the specification should say which components are treated rather than applying the requirement to a whole package.

Projects inside China work to a different system. GB 8624 classifies materials as A, B1, B2 or B3, and GB 20286 requires flame-retardant board products used in places of public assembly to reach at least GB 8624 B1. A Chinese B1 certificate and a Euroclass report are not interchangeable, and a project that has to satisfy both needs both documents in the handover pack.

Moisture-resistant board: what it resists, and what it does not

Moisture-resistant MDF is the same fibreboard with a different resin system, and the performance difference is set out in EN 622-5 as a separate type with its own test regime. The gap is not a marketing margin.

Property at 18 mmStandard MDFMDF.HWhat it means on site
24h thickness swelling, EN 31712% max8% max18 mm swells about 2.2 mm against 1.4 mm
Internal bond, EN 3190.55 N/mm20.75 N/mm2Higher resin content, better screw holding
Bending strength, EN 31020 N/mm224 N/mm2Less sag on a long shelf
Swelling after cyclic test, EN 321Not required15% maxOnly MDF.H is tested wet, dry, wet
Internal bond after cyclic testNot required0.20 N/mm2The figure that predicts real service life

The cyclic figures are the ones to ask for. Standard MDF is never subjected to the EN 321 wet-dry-wet cycle, so a supplier quoting only the 24-hour swelling number has quoted the easier half of the test.

A green core is an identifier, not a performance claim. Several mills dye the core of boards that meet no humid-condition class at all, and colour alone proves nothing. What establishes MDF.H is the type declaration together with the cyclic results, and both should be in the order documents rather than in a sales conversation.

What MDF.H resists is high relative humidity and occasional wetting. What it does not resist is standing or running water. A shower interior, a steam room or a poolside deck needs a waterproof substrate such as a cement or mineral core board, with every cut edge sealed. Used in those locations, MDF.H swells at the first unsealed edge, and the failure appears as a swollen bottom rail rather than as a visible crack.

The rule that applies to all of them: seal every cut edge. A moisture-resistant board with an unsealed edge performs like a standard board at that edge, because that is exactly what it has become.

Ten rules, none of which costs money to follow at the machining stage and all of which cost money to correct after installation. The screw edge distance and the four-edge banding are the two that most often get skipped under schedule pressure.

What to write in the purchase order

A board specification that survives procurement has to name the type, not the family, and has to name the test that proves it. Six lines cover it.

  • Type and standard. “Particleboard to EN 312, type P2” rather than “particleboard”. “MDF to EN 622-5, type MDF.H” rather than “moisture-resistant MDF”.
  • Mechanical values. Internal bond, bending strength and 24-hour thickness swelling, each with its EN test method. A grade name without a value is an assertion.
  • Formaldehyde class by market. E1 to EN 717-1 for Europe, CARB Phase 2 or TSCA Title VI to ASTM D6007 for the United States, and the class the destination actually enforces rather than the one the mill prefers.
  • Reaction to fire. The Euroclass with both suffixes, plus the classification report number. For China, the GB 8624 class and the GB 20286 requirement if the building is a place of public assembly.
  • Moisture content at dispatch, matched to the destination climate rather than to factory habit.
  • Edge treatment. Banding thickness and coverage, and whether cut edges on concealed parts are sealed.

The same discipline applies to laminate. “HPL to EN 438-3, grade HGS, 0.8 mm, density 1.35 g/cm3 minimum, boiling water resistance rating 5” is an order. “Fire board top” is a conversation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between P2 and P5 particleboard?

P2 is bound with UF resin and rated for dry interior fitments including furniture. P5 uses MUF resin, carries a green-dyed core and is rated for humid conditions while carrying load. On an 18 mm board, internal bond rises from 0.35 to 0.45 N/mm2 and bending strength from 11 to 16 N/mm2. P5 costs 15 to 25 percent more. A wardrobe in a conditioned guestroom is P2 work; a bathroom vanity carcass is P5 work.

Does EN 622-5 cover HDF?

No. EN 622-5 defines MDF, MDF.H, MDF.LA, MDF.HLS, L-MDF and UL-MDF with performance requirements for each. EN 316 defines HDF by density at 800 kg/m3 and above, but EN 622-5 has no HDF type, so there is no harmonised class to test an HDF board against. An HDF claim rests on the mill data sheet, which is why the data sheet belongs in the order documents.

Is 防火板 the same as fire-retardant board?

No. In Chinese factory usage 防火板 almost always means high-pressure laminate, the decorative phenolic-faced surface specified under EN 438. A fire-retardant board is a substrate treated or manufactured to reach a Euroclass B or C rating under EN 13501-1. One is a surface, the other is a core, and a specification that does not say which cannot be priced or inspected.

What does a green core mean on MDF?

It is an identifier, not a performance claim. Mills dye the core of boards that meet no humid-condition class at all. What establishes MDF.H is the EN 622-5 type declaration together with the EN 321 cyclic test results, which standard MDF is never subjected to. Ask for the declaration and the cyclic figures, not the colour.

Can MDF.H be used inside a shower?

No. MDF.H is rated for humid interior conditions, meaning high relative humidity and occasional wetting, not standing or running water. A shower interior, steam room or poolside deck needs a waterproof substrate such as cement or mineral core board with all cut edges sealed.

What thickness swelling is acceptable for hotel furniture board?

At 18 mm, EN 622-5 requires 24-hour swelling at or below 12 percent for standard MDF and 8 percent for MDF.H. Particleboard is specified through its EN 312 grade rather than a single figure. Anything swelling more than 2 mm on an 18 mm board has lost the flatness a door or drawer front needs.

Which board should a hotel wardrobe carcass use?

Particleboard P2 at 18 mm for sides, shelves and backs, or MDF where the carcass is veneered. Both are rated for dry interiors, and a guestroom is a dry interior once the HVAC is running. The plinth, which sits near floor moisture, and any carcass in a bathroom or utility room are the parts that need P5 or MDF.H.
