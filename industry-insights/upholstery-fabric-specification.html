Hotel Upholstery Fabric: How to Read the Numbers Before You Approve the Sample

foshanFFE.com · October 30, 2026 · 14 min read

Two fabrics arrive in the same sample bag. One is quoted at 45,000 rubs and costs USD 9 a metre. The other is quoted at 28,000 rubs and costs USD 18. The cheaper one looks like the better buy, and on the specification sheet it reads as the more durable choice.

Six months into operation the 45,000-rub fabric is pilling across every seat pan in the lobby, and the seams on the banquette have opened. The 28,000-rub fabric is still tight. The number that decided the purchase measured one thing and the failure came from something else.

Abrasion resistance is the most quoted figure in upholstery and the least useful on its own. It describes what happens when an abrasive pad moves across the face of a fabric. It does not describe pilling, colour loss, seam failure, foam collapse, or what happens when a guest sits down with a wet swimsuit. A specification that names only a rub count leaves five failure modes unaddressed.

What follows is the test set that actually predicts service life in a hotel, the thresholds worth writing into an RFQ, and the checks that separate a real test report from a document that was generated to close a purchase order.

What does the Martindale number actually measure?

Martindale abrasion is defined in ISO 12947-1 and 12947-2, and in ASTM D4966 for the US market. A specimen is clamped and rubbed against an abradant under a fixed load, tracing a Lissajous figure so the direction of abrasion keeps changing. For upholstery the load is 12 kPa. For apparel and lighter fabrics it is 9 kPa, and a report quoting 9 kPa figures for a contract seating fabric is reporting the wrong test.

The abradant matters as much as the load. Wool abradant is gentler and produces higher numbers. Wire mesh is harsher. Two laboratories testing the same fabric with different abradants will publish results a third apart, and both will be correct.

The end-point matters more. ISO 12947-2 stops at thread break, defined as two broken yarns in the fabric structure. Some suppliers stop at appearance change instead, which is a softer criterion and inflates the figure. A report that does not state which criterion was applied cannot be compared with anything.

North America uses a different machine. Wyzenbeek, ASTM D4157, rubs the fabric back and forth over a curved surface with cotton duck or wire mesh, and counts one complete return as a double rub. Martindale counts a single cycle. The two numbers are not convertible, and the industry convention that treats 15,000 Wyzenbeek as roughly equivalent to 25,000 Martindale is a rule of thumb rather than a conversion factor.

Which abrasion band should a hotel specification require?

BS EN 14465 Annex A sets three performance levels for upholstery fabrics: level A at 35,000 cycles and above, level B from 12,000 to 30,000, and level C from 4,000 to 10,000. The older BS 2543 classification splits further, with light domestic flat weave at 15,000 and general domestic at 20,000. Neither standard was written for hotels. Both describe minimum floors for furniture in public use, and a hotel lobby exceeds that description by an order of magnitude.

The practical approach is to set the band by where the piece sits and how many times a day a body lands on it. A guestroom desk chair sees two uses a day. A lobby lounge chair in a 200-key property sees two hundred. The fabric that holds in the first will fail in the second, and no amount of specification writing changes that.

The rub count trap. A fabric at 100,000 cycles with a weak weave structure will fail at the seam before it fails on the face. Seam slippage, not surface abrasion, is the leading cause of upholstery failure in hotel seating, and it is not measured by the Martindale test at all.

Which tests beyond abrasion decide whether the fabric survives?

Seven further properties determine whether a fabric holds up in a hotel. Each has a standard, a threshold, and a visible failure mode. A specification that names all seven is roughly four times as long as one that names only abrasion, and it is the difference between a fabric that reaches the next renovation cycle and one that is reupholstered inside three years.

Coated fabrics and leather carry different thresholds

Polyurethane and vinyl-coated fabrics are tested for abrasion under ISO 5470-2 rather than ISO 12947, and European public procurement commonly sets the floor at 75,000 cycles. Coated fabrics do not pill and do not fray at the cut edge, which allows a narrower seam allowance, but they fail differently: by cracking at flex folds and by delaminating where the coating separates from the backing cloth. Flex resistance, tested to ISO 7854, predicts that failure better than any abrasion figure.

Leather is measured on its own scale. EN 13336 sets the reference values used across European contract furniture: tear load at 20 N or above under EN ISO 3377-1, dry finish adhesion at 2 N per 10 mm or above under EN ISO 11644, and colour fastness to rubbing under EN ISO 11640 evaluated for both colour change and staining of the felt. A leather quoted only by thickness has not been specified at all.

Which fire standard applies to which market?

Fire performance is the one specification item that cannot be traded off against cost, because failure removes the product from the market rather than shortening its life. The standard that applies is set by the destination, not by the factory's preference, and the test is run on the fabric and foam combination as assembled, not on the fabric alone.

What foam and seam specification goes with the fabric?

Fabric is the visible layer and the one buyers spend time on. Foam and stitching decide whether the seat still looks like a seat in year four. A 50,000-cycle fabric wrapped over collapsing foam produces a wrinkled, hollow-looking chair, and the fabric will be blamed for it.

Seat foam for contract seating runs at 35 to 45 kg/m3 density with an indentation load deflection between 130 and 180 N measured at 25 percent compression under ISO 2439. Back cushions drop to 25 to 30 kg/m3 and 70 to 100 N because they carry less load and benefit from softness. Front edges and armrests need 45 to 55 kg/m3, because that narrow band takes the hand, the knee and the trolley impact that the seat pan never sees.

Compression set is the number that predicts sag. Tested under ISO 1856 at 70 C for 22 hours, contract foam should hold at 5 percent or below. Foam that reads 8 to 10 percent will lose a visible amount of height within two years of lobby use. Resilience under ISO 8307 should stay at 40 percent or above.

Stitch density on load-bearing seams should reach 8 stitches per inch minimum, with 10 preferred on seat platforms and any seam running across the front edge of a cushion. Below 6 stitches per inch the seam becomes the weakest line in the assembly, and a seam that opens cannot be repaired invisibly. Welt cord, if specified, should be sewn rather than glued, because glued welt separates at the flex point within a season.

How to read a fabric test report before you trust it

A test report is a claim about a specific piece of fabric at a specific moment. Four things determine whether it supports the goods on the container.

The article and batch. The report must name the exact article number and the batch or lot tested. A report naming a fabric family, or a colourway the factory no longer produces, covers nothing. Fabric performance varies between dye lots because the yarn and the finishing tension vary.

The abradant and the load. Wire mesh and wool produce different results on the same fabric. A 9 kPa load produces a higher number than 12 kPa. Both are legitimate tests and neither is the test the specification called for if the specification named a different one.

The end-point criterion. Thread break and appearance change are both defensible and they are not the same number. A report that stops at appearance change has measured the fabric's appearance, not its structural survival.

The laboratory and its accreditation. The report should carry an accreditation number under ISO/IEC 17025. Where a factory cannot produce an accredited report for the exact fabric, the honest position is that the fabric is untested, not that it passed.

A report dated before the production run does not cover that run. Ask for the report date and the fabric delivery date side by side. If the mill changed yarn supplier between them, the earlier report describes a fabric that is no longer being made.

What does fabric cost across the bands?

Fabric pricing tracks three variables: fibre, weave density and whether the performance comes from the fibre itself or from a finish applied afterwards. Solution-dyed acrylic carries its colour inside the fibre, so light fastness does not depend on a coating that can wear away. A topical stain treatment on a cheap polyester does the opposite.

The spread between the entry and high-performance bands is roughly four to one. On a 300-chair lobby and restaurant package, that spread is a five-figure decision, and it is the one place in an FF&E budget where a lower grade produces a visible failure that guests photograph.

How should fabric be inspected on arrival?

Fabric inspection follows AQL 2.5 for major defects and AQL 4.0 for minor, on a roll-by-roll basis under adequate lighting at a defined viewing distance. Four checks catch most of what goes wrong.

Shade continuity across rolls within a dye lot, and a separate check between lots. Fabric from two lots will not match under hotel lighting even when both match the swatch, and the mismatch shows on a banquette where two rolls meet.

Roll direction. Fabric is rolled face-in or face-out, and a mixed consignment produces panels that catch light differently. Confirm the direction in the purchase order and check it on arrival.

Width and length against the packing list, allowing for the standard tolerance. Short rolls cost more than they appear to, because the shortfall is discovered during cutting when the remainder cannot be reordered in the same lot.

Retain a sealed sample from each lot, signed and dated, held until the project's defects liability period ends. That sample is the only physical evidence of what was approved, and it settles most disputes about colour and hand feel without a laboratory.

Specifying upholstery for a hotel package?

Send the FF&E schedule and the destination market. Test reports for the exact fabrics and foams quoted, matched to the fire standard that market requires, come back with the quotation.

Frequently asked questions

What Martindale rub count should hotel upholstery have?

Lobby and bar seating at 50,000 cycles and above. Restaurant dining and guestroom desk chairs at 30,000 to 40,000. Banquet and stacking chairs hold at 25,000 because use is intermittent. Below 20,000 is residential territory. BS EN 14465 Annex A sets the formal bands at 35,000 for level A, 12,000 to 30,000 for level B, and 4,000 to 10,000 for level C.

Is Martindale the same as Wyzenbeek?

No, and the figures do not convert. Martindale rubs a Lissajous path at 12 kPa and counts cycles. Wyzenbeek rubs straight back and forth and counts each return as a double rub. The convention treating 15,000 Wyzenbeek as roughly 25,000 Martindale is a rule of thumb. A quotation stating a number without naming the method has not specified anything.

Which fire standard applies to hotel upholstery?

California and US projects use CAL TB 117-2013 for components and TB 133 where a complete seating unit must pass in a public area. UK contract work uses BS 7176 at the required hazard category against BS 5852 fire sources, with Crib 5 the usual hotel floor. EU projects use EN 1021-1 and EN 1021-2. Marine projects use IMO FTP Code Part 8.

What is a good pilling result for contract fabric?

Grade 4 or better on ISO 12945-2 after 2,000 and 7,000 rubs, rated against the photographic standard. Grade 3 is where seat pans begin to look worn in year two. Wool blends and loose-spun chenille pill most; tightly spun polyester and solution-dyed acrylic pill least.

How can a buyer tell whether a fabric test report is genuine?

Four checks. It must name the exact article and batch. It must name the abradant and the load. It must state the end-point criterion. And the laboratory must carry an ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation number. A report dated before the production run does not cover that run.

What foam density goes with hotel contract upholstery?

Seats at 35 to 45 kg/m3 with ILD of 130 to 180 N. Backs at 25 to 30 kg/m3 and 70 to 100 N. Front edges and armrests at 45 to 55 kg/m3. Compression set should hold at 5 percent or below after 22 hours at 70 C.

How much does hotel upholstery fabric cost from China?

Indicative FOB at 54 inch width: basic polyester USD 3 to 7 per metre, blended mid-grade USD 7 to 14, high-performance solution-dyed acrylic and dense contract weaves USD 15 to 32, coated and polyurethane USD 12 to 28, semi-aniline leather USD 3.5 to 7 per square foot. Fabric runs 25 to 40 percent of an upholstered chair's FOB cost.
