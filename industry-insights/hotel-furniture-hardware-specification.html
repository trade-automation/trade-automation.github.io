Hotel Furniture Hardware: Hinges, Runners and Handles That Survive a Hotel

foshanFFE.com · October 30, 2026 · 15 min read

Eighteen months after opening, one in three wardrobe doors in a new hotel is hanging low enough to catch on the carcass. The hinge is not broken. The hinge cup has pulled out of the panel, taking a plug of particleboard with it.

The supplier produces a test report. The hinge passed EN 15570. The document is genuine, the laboratory is accredited, and the report is also useless, because EN 15570 has three performance levels and the report is for level 1: 20,000 cycles, tested against a light door.

The hotel door weighs twice what the test door weighed and opens four to six times a day. The standard was met. The application was not.

Hardware is the cheapest part of a furniture package and the most frequent cause of a maintenance call. It is also the easiest thing to specify properly, because unlike finish quality or upholstery hand, hardware performance is fully documented in numbers that a purchase order can reference.

Hinges: what EN 15570 actually grades

EN 15570 covers hinges pivoting on a vertical axis, the concealed cup hinge being the type in almost every hotel wardrobe and vanity. The standard sets test methods and requirements for strength and durability, plus maximum opening and closing forces and a maximum permitted door sag after the durability test.

What matters for specification is that the durability test runs at one of three performance levels, and the cycle count differs by a factor of four between the lowest and the highest.

Performance levelDurability cycles, clause 6.3.7Designed forHotel verdict
Level 120,000Light domestic furniture, small cabinet doorsNot acceptable. Fails in guestroom use
Level 240,000Kitchen wall units, bathroom cabinetsMinimum for back-of-house storage
Level 380,000Large wardrobe doors, heavy fronts, contract useRequired for guestroom and public area

Table 1. The same standard number covers all three levels. A report that says "tested to EN 15570" without naming the level is not a specification, it is a certificate of having been tested at some level.

The standard also separates the test samples into three groups: one set for overload tests, one for functional tests, one for the corrosion test. Corrosion is therefore a discrete pass or fail item inside the same standard, and it is worth asking which salt spray duration was applied, because EN 15570 does not set a single figure for all environments.

How many hinges a door needs

Door height drives hinge count, and door weight decides whether the hinge is rated for the job at all. Two hinges on a 2-metre wardrobe door is a design error that no hinge will survive.

Table 2. Hinge count by door height. The second failure behind low performance level is simply too few hinges on a tall door.

Two further details belong on the drawing. The hinge plate spacing should put the outermost hinges close to the top and bottom edges, within 100 mm, so the door cannot lever against a single fixing. And the cup bore depth must leave enough material behind the cup: a 35 mm cup in a 16 mm door leaves almost nothing, which is why 18 mm is the practical minimum panel for a hinged door in contract work.

Drawer runners: EN 15338 levels and load classes

EN 15338 covers extension elements and their components, which in furniture terms means drawer runners and pull-out mechanisms. Like the hinge standard, it grades performance into levels, and it separately rates the load the runner can carry, stated in kilograms against a 500 mm reference length.

Performance levelLoad classes availableDurability testWhere it belongs
Level 110 to 25 kgShortest cycle countBedside drawer, occasional use
Level 230 kg50,000 cyclesGuestroom wardrobe, desk and nightstand drawers
Level 340 to 70 kgLongest cycle countMinibar, luggage rack pull-out, heavy storage

Table 3. EN 15338 grades performance and load separately. A runner can be level 3 and still be rated at 10 kg, so both figures have to appear in the specification.

Load rating scales with length. A runner rated 40 kg at 500 mm will not carry 40 kg at 650 mm, because the lever arm grows and the rail deflects further. Any quotation stating a load figure should also state the nominal length it applies to.

EN 15338 also carries a corrosion requirement, commonly met through a condensation water test to EN 6270 rather than a salt spray test. That is a weaker check than NSS and is one reason the salt spray figure should be specified separately rather than assumed from a runner's EN 15338 report.

Handles: material, load and the Zamak question

Handles fail in two ways: the fixing pulls out of the door, or the finish degrades until the handle looks older than the furniture. Both are decided by material and by how the handle is attached, not by how it looks in a sample bag.

MaterialProcessStrengthRelative costHotel suitability
Zamak 3Die-cast zinc alloyModerateBaselineResidential. Not for contract
Zamak 5Die-cast zinc with copper additionHigher strength and hardnessPlus 3 to 5 percent on the castingCorrect grade for hotel hardware
Die-cast aluminiumDie-castLower, but lightLowDecorative, low load, large format
Stainless 304WroughtHighHigherWet areas, general interior
Stainless 316WroughtHighHighestCoastal, pool, chlorine exposure
Solid brassWrought or castHighHighestPremium public area, lobby

Table 5. The Zamak 3 to Zamak 5 step costs three to five percent of the raw casting. A single warranty call to replace a handle costs ten to twenty times that difference, before the room is taken out of service.

Fixing decides the other half. A pull handle fixed at one point rotates under load and eventually loosens; two fixing points, with thread locking compound on machine screws, is the contract baseline. Bolt-through fixing from the inside face is stronger than a wood screw into the door skin and is worth specifying on any handle that will be pulled rather than pushed.

Static load is the number to put in the purchase order: 500 N applied horizontally for one minute with no permanent deformation. That is a low bar for a well-made handle and a reliable way to reject a hollow casting.

Corrosion: how many salt spray hours

Salt spray testing accelerates atmospheric corrosion, roughly fifty to a hundred times, in a chamber of 5 percent sodium chloride mist at 35 degrees. ISO 9227 NSS and ASTM B117 describe the same procedure. The result is expressed as hours to a defined failure, and the failure definition is where specifications are won and lost.

EnvironmentNSS hoursAcceptance ratingNote
Air-conditioned interior, bedroom, corridor48No red rust, ASTM B537 grade 9 or betterEuropean retail baseline for bathroom fittings
General hotel interior, lobby, restaurant96SameStandard contract figure
Bathroom, vanity, mirror frame200Same, white corrosion limited to 5 percent of visible areaHumidity and cleaning chemicals
Coastal site within 1 km200 to 480Same316 stainless or PVD coating
Pool, spa, chlorine atmosphere480 and aboveSame316 stainless mandatory
Marine exterior720Same, plus IMO where applicableSpecify alloy, not just hours

Table 6. Hours without a rating are an incomplete specification. A part can survive 96 hours and still show white corrosion across the whole visible face, which is a fail on a handle and a pass on a test report.

Plating thickness is the underlying variable. Decorative hardware should carry at least 20 micrometres of total plating, with a copper strike under the nickel and a chromium topcoat of 0.2 to 0.3 micrometres. Measured by magnetic gauge to ISO 2178, on a sample, at goods-in.

Why plated parts fail early: four causes, in order of frequency. Total plating below 20 micrometres. No copper strike, so the nickel layer is porous and the zinc beneath it corrodes through. Casting porosity that traps plating solution and blows the coating from underneath. And cut or unplated edges, which corrode first and are usually hidden inside the fixing.

Table 7. Five failure modes cover most hardware warranty claims. Each has a cause that can be specified out of the product before the order is placed.

The soft-close damper deserves separate attention because it is the most common mismatch. A hinge rated 80,000 cycles can carry a damper rated 30,000. The hinge will still be serviceable when the door starts slamming, and the maintenance team will replace the whole hinge because the damper is integral. Specify the damper life as a number, not as "soft close".

Hardware is inspected before it is fitted, not after. Most of the checks are documentary, which means they can be done from a desk, and the physical checks take minutes on a sample.

The function test matters more than it looks. Ten hinges and ten runners from different cartons will expose a mixed batch faster than any document, because a substituted part usually arrives with the same label and a different feel.

Guestroom and public area packages quoted with hinge level, runner class, alloy grade and salt spray hours named in the offer, so hardware performance is agreed before the first production run.

Frequently asked questions

What does EN 15570 actually certify on a hinge?

It certifies strength and durability at one of three performance levels. Level 1 runs 20,000 open and close cycles, level 2 runs 40,000 and level 3 runs 80,000. The standard also sets maximum opening and closing forces and a maximum permitted door sag after the durability test. A supplier who says the hinge passes EN 15570 without naming the level has told the buyer nothing, because level 1 and level 3 carry the same standard number and four times the cycle count apart.

Which hinge level should a hotel specify?

Level 3, at 80,000 cycles. A guestroom wardrobe door opens four to six times a day, which is roughly 2,000 cycles a year, so 80,000 covers a fifteen to twenty year life with margin for the doors that get used harder. Level 2 at 40,000 is acceptable for back-of-house storage. Level 1 belongs in residential furniture and will fail in a hotel.

What is the difference between EN 15570 and EN 15338?

EN 15570 covers hinges pivoting on a vertical axis. EN 15338 covers extension elements, which in practice means drawer runners and pull-out mechanisms. Both grade performance into levels and both test strength and durability plus corrosion, and both require the report to state which level was tested. A hardware package needs both standards covered, not one or the other.

How many salt spray hours does hotel furniture hardware need?

Ninety-six hours of neutral salt spray with no red rust for general interior use, and 200 hours for bathrooms and other humid areas. Coastal sites within a kilometre of the sea should be at 200 to 480 hours, and pool or spa areas at 480 hours and above with 316 stainless. The hours mean nothing on their own, because a part can pass 96 hours with white corrosion across the visible face. Specify the hours and the rating together, for example ASTM B537 grade 9.

Is Zamak 3 acceptable for hotel furniture hardware?

Zamak 3 is a residential die-casting alloy. Hotel applications should specify Zamak 5, which carries a small copper addition that raises strength and hardness. The cost difference is three to five percent on the raw casting, against a service call that costs ten to twenty times that difference. The material grade belongs on the purchase order and should be backed by a mill certificate from the alloy supplier.

Why do hinge cups pull out of the cabinet panel?

Because the screw has less to grip than the load requires. Particleboard holds a screw far less firmly than solid timber, and a short screw in an 18 mm panel leaves very little engagement. The fixes are a panel of at least 18 mm at the hinge position, Euro screws long enough to reach panel thickness plus six millimetres, and hinge plates spread far enough apart that no single fixing takes the whole door weight.

Should the purchase order name hardware brands?

Naming a brand is the simplest enforceable specification, because it removes the argument about what level was tested. Where a brand cannot be named for cost or availability reasons, specify the standard, the performance level, the load class, the salt spray hours with rating, the alloy grade and the cycle life of any damper. That combination is harder for a substitute part to meet than a brand name is to counterfeit.
