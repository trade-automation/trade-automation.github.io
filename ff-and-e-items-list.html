The Full List of FF&E Items
for a Hotel Project

Every item a hotel FF&E package has to cover, grouped by area, for developers and procurement teams writing a scope of works

Start With the Guestroom List

The Short Answer

What Is on an FF&E Items List?

A hotel FF&E items list names every movable or semi-permanent item that stays with the building: guestroom casegoods, beds and headboards, seating, lighting, mattresses, drapery, bath vanities and mirrors, lobby and restaurant furniture, wellness equipment, outdoor furniture, artwork and signage. Linens, towels, tableware and small appliances belong on the OS&E list instead.

The list below is written the way a scope of works is normally written: by area, from the guestroom outward to public areas, outdoor spaces and back of house. Each row names the items a buyer has to specify, price and inspect. Use it as the starting point when compiling a list of FF&E items for hotels, then cut it down to the areas the project actually has.

Two boundaries cause most of the scope gaps on hotel projects. The first is FF&E against OS&E. The second is FF&E against the base building. Both are worth settling before a single quotation is requested.

Scope Boundaries

Four Lists, Not One

A hotel is furnished from four separate lists. Mixing them up is the fastest way to discover a gap two weeks before installation.

List What sits on it How it behaves
FF&E Casegoods, seating, lighting, mattresses, drapery, vanities, mirrors, public-area furniture, outdoor furniture, artwork, signage Capitalised, stays with the building, one-off purchase
OS&E Linens, towels, tableware, glassware, small appliances, housekeeping equipment, guest amenities, uniforms Expensed, replaced on an operating cycle
Base building Structure, MEP, tiling, ceilings, doors and frames, plumbing rough-in, and sanitaryware on some contracts Carried by the construction contract, priced by the main contractor
Specialist packages Kitchen and laundry equipment, A/V and IT, and on some projects fitness equipment Quoted by the specialist suppliers who install and service them

An FF&E integrator works the first list end to end and flags anything that lands on the other three, so the buyer is not holding an incomplete scope when the quotation arrives.

Back-of-house furniture is the list most often left out of the main package. It then arrives as a second order with a second freight booking, at a higher unit price.

Working Method

How to Turn This List Into a Scope

Four steps take the list from a reading exercise to a document a factory can price.

  • Tag every row FF&E or OS&E. One column on the schedule prevents the classic gap where the buyer assumes towels are in the package.
  • Give every item a specification number. Finish, dimension and quantity per item, keyed to the room type matrix, so a quotation can be compared line by line.
  • Write down what the base building carries. Sanitaryware, recessed lighting, life-safety signage and broadloom carpet are the four items that most often fall between contracts.
  • Decide the artwork route early. Art, mirrors and accessories can run inside the FF&E package or through an art consultant. Choosing late delays the whole loading schedule.

A package assembled under one purchase order consolidates into a single loading window: a 300-room scope typically fits 15–25 containers loaded within five days. The hotel FF&E checklist covers the stage-by-stage version of the same work, and cost per key covers what each tier of list should cost.

FF&E covers the furniture, fixtures and equipment that stay with the building: guestroom casegoods, beds and headboards, seating, tables, lighting, mattresses, drapery, bath vanities, mirrors, lobby and restaurant furniture, wellness and outdoor furniture, artwork and signage. The test is simple. If it is installed as part of the property and capitalised with the project, it is FF&E. If it is consumed or replaced on an operating cycle, it is OS&E.

OS&E covers operating supplies and equipment: linens, towels, tableware, glassware, small appliances, housekeeping equipment, guest amenities and uniforms. Those items are expensed and replaced regularly. FF&E is capitalised and bought once, or once per renovation cycle. The two lists are normally contracted separately, and the split should be written into the schedule rather than left to interpretation.

Vanity units, countertops, mirrors, mirror lighting, shower screens, bath panels and bath accessories are FF&E. On some contracts the sanitaryware moves into FF&E, so confirm the split at RFQ stage instead of assuming either way.
