US Hotel Construction Pipeline 2026: $895M+ FF&E Opportunity for Global Suppliers

Published August 29, 2026  |  8 min read  |  By foshanFFE.com Editorial Team

The United States hotel construction pipeline has reached historic levels. As of Q2 2026, there are 5,975 projects encompassing 703,001 rooms in various stages of development — from early planning to active construction. For global FF&E (Furniture, Fixtures & Equipment) suppliers and integrators, this translates into one of the most significant procurement windows in the past decade.

With 661 hotels forecast to open in 2026 alone, and an additional 1,570 hotels undergoing renovation or conversion, the FF&E demand is not just substantial — it is sustained and measurable. This article breaks down the numbers and explains what they mean for suppliers looking to capture share in the world's largest hospitality market.

$895M – $1.5B in FF&E Spend

The combined renovation and conversion pipeline of 255,834 rooms, at an average of $3,500–$6,000 per room for guestroom furniture alone, represents nearly $1 billion in immediate FF&E replacement demand — before accounting for public area furnishings, lighting, or bathroom vanities.

The Renovation & Conversion Wave

Beyond new construction, the US market is experiencing a record wave of hotel renovations and brand conversions. In 2026, there are 1,567 conversion projects (hotels switching brands) — up 15% year-over-year. When combined with full-scale renovations, the total reaches 2,097 projects covering 255,834 rooms.

For FF&E suppliers, conversion projects are particularly attractive because:

  • They require complete furniture replacement to meet new brand standards
  • Timelines are compressed — often 8–14 weeks from PO to delivery
  • Standardized room layouts enable efficient manufacturing and packaging
  • Volume pricing opportunities exist across multiple-room packages

A typical 200-room conversion project generates $1.2M–$2.5M in total FF&E spend, including casegoods, soft seating, lobby furniture, lighting fixtures, and bathroom vanities. For sourcing integrators based in manufacturing hubs like Foshan, China, these projects represent ideal engagement opportunities.

Luxury Segment Hits Record Highs

The luxury hotel segment is experiencing unprecedented growth. With 103 projects and 25,496 rooms in the pipeline — up 12% year-over-year — luxury brand expansions are driving demand for premium FF&E specifications.

Notable 2026 openings include:

  • Kimpton Era Midtown, New York — 529 rooms, Manhattan's largest new city hotel
  • Cincinnati Marriott Convention Center Hotel — 700 rooms, $325M construction project
  • Four Seasons Resort The Biltmore, Santa Barbara — 138 rooms, luxury resort conversion
  • The Vineta Hotel, Palm Beach — 41 rooms, Oetker Collection ultra-luxury opening

These projects command higher per-room FF&E budgets ($8,000–$15,000+ per room) and require suppliers who can deliver custom millwork, premium upholstery, and coordinated design packages at scale.

The Federal Procurement Channel

An often-overlooked avenue for FF&E suppliers is the US federal government procurement system. Through SAM.gov and GSA schedules, federal agencies procure furniture for military lodging facilities, VA hospitality suites, and government-managed hotels.

Key NAICS codes for hotel FF&E include 337121 (upholstered household furniture), 337122 (nonupholstered wood household furniture), and 337121P (packaged furniture for lodging/quarters). Registration on GSA's Multiple Award Schedule provides pre-qualified supplier status, enabling direct response to federal solicitations.

While the federal channel requires compliance documentation and competitive pricing structures, it offers recurring demand and long-term contract stability that commercial projects may not provide.

What This Means for Global FF&E Suppliers

The US pipeline data tells a clear story: demand for hotel FF&E is not cyclical — it is structural. Demographic shifts, brand proliferation, and the conversion wave are creating sustained procurement needs that extend through 2028 and beyond.

For international suppliers and sourcing integrators, the opportunity requires navigating a 28–30% tariff environment (including Section 301 duties). However, with a 30–45% cost advantage from Foshan-based manufacturing, even after tariff absorption, the landed cost remains competitive against domestic sourcing for most furniture categories.

Success in this market requires:

  • Understanding CAL 117 fire compliance requirements for upholstery
  • Offering DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) pricing that includes tariff costs
  • Demonstrating capacity for compressed timelines (8–14 weeks PO-to-site)
  • Providing single-source integration across casegoods, soft seating, lighting, and bath vanities
