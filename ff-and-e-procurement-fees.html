The Short Answer

FF&E Procurement Fee Structures

FF&E procurement is charged in one of four ways: a percentage of the FF&E budget, cost-plus on the invoice value, a fixed fee per project or per key, or no separate fee at all where an integrator carries the margin inside the FOB unit price. Percentage-based proposals are commonly quoted in the 8–15% range of the FF&E budget, sliding down as the budget grows.

The structure matters more than the rate. A low percentage on a budget figure that includes items the agent will not actually buy is not a low fee. A fixed fee that excludes inspection and freight coordination is not fixed. Read the structure first, then the number.

Treat any percentage band as a starting point for the conversation rather than a benchmark. What the fee covers is the part that decides the final cost.

The Models

Five Ways Procurement Gets Priced

Model How It Is Charged When It Fits
Percentage of FF&E budget A percentage of the FF&E budget, commonly quoted in the 8–15% range, with the rate falling as the budget grows Larger programmes where the owner wants the fee ring-fenced and visible as a separate line
Cost-plus Factory invoice value plus a fixed markup, commonly a mid single-digit to low double-digit percentage Projects with a defined specification where the owner wants full visibility of the factory price
Fixed fee A lump sum per project, or a fee per key, agreed before the work starts Projects with a frozen scope where both sides want the number settled up front
Time and materials An hourly or daily rate for the work actually performed Feasibility and tender-stage support, or an owner who intends to run the delivery in-house
Integrator margin in FOB price No separate procurement fee. The margin sits inside the FOB unit price for each item Owners who want one supplier of record, one purchase order and one warranty contact

The first three models put a visible number next to the word procurement. The last one does not, which makes it harder to compare and easier to misread. The section below sets out how to read it.

Coverage

What a Procurement Fee Usually Covers

Scope of service is the real difference between two proposals at the same rate. These are the tasks that sit inside a procurement mandate, and the ones that usually sit outside it.

Task Usually Inside the Fee Usually Outside the Fee
Tender management Bid list, scope of work, price comparison and clarification rounds Redesign of the specification to reduce the price
Contract administration Purchase order drafting, milestone tracking, supplier correspondence Legal review of the contract terms
Sampling coordination Scheduling prototypes and tracking approvals against the frozen specification Repeat prototype rounds caused by a late design change
Quality control Factory inspections and pre-shipment inspection reports Third-party laboratory testing beyond the agreed standard set
Logistics coordination Consolidation planning, loading supervision and document checking Freight, duty, expedited shipping and site storage
Installation Scheduling and snag list administration The installation labour itself, unless it is priced into the package
Warranty administration Claim handling against the purchase order Rework caused by site damage after handover

Two exclusions cause most disputes. Expedited freight is rarely inside a fee, and it is rarely avoidable when a design change lands late. Repeat prototypes are the other one. Both are cheaper to define at the contract stage than to argue about at the loading dock.

Our Model

How foshanFFE Charges

foshanFFE works as a hospitality FF&E integrator, and the commercial model follows from that. There is no separate procurement service fee. The margin sits inside the FOB unit price of each item.

In practice the buyer sees one unit price per line item, with the specification it assumes. Comparing it with another quotation is a like-for-like exercise at the item level: same dimensions, same finish, same foam and fabric build, same test documentation, same packing. The coordination work that a percentage fee would pay for, from factory allocation through inspection, consolidation and warranty, is carried by the integrator as part of the delivery obligation.

  • No percentage on the FF&E budget. The buyer is not charged a fee that grows as the specification grows.
  • One supplier of record. Guestroom and public-area packages are delivered on one purchase order through a 19-factory partner network in Foshan.
  • One warranty point. Claims are handled by the supplier of record rather than routed between factories.
  • Documented scope. Each price states what it includes, so the comparison against another quote is made on scope rather than on a headline rate.

This model suits owners who want a single accountable supplier and a price they can compare per item. It suits owners less well if they want to see the factory invoice and pay a markup on top of it, because the factory pricing is not the offer.

Due Diligence

What to Check in a Fee Proposal

  • Charged on budget or on actual spend. The two diverge when the scope shrinks. Ask which figure the percentage is applied to, and what happens if the project is reduced.
  • What the fee excludes. Freight, duty, installation, expedited shipping, site storage, laboratory testing and repeat prototypes are the usual exclusions.
  • Whether inspection is inside or outside. A fee that excludes pre-shipment inspection is not covering the risk that matters most.
  • Who carries rework. If a factory delivers to a specification the agent approved, who pays to put it right.
  • Warranty administration. Whether the fee covers claims for the full warranty period, or only until handover.
  • Change order treatment. The rate applied to scope added after the contract is signed.

Two proposals at the same percentage can differ by a third once exclusions are priced in. The hotel FF&E checklist covers the stages those exclusions sit in, and cost per key covers the budget the percentage would be applied to.

The right question is which model puts the delivery risk on the party best able to carry it.
