The Problem

15 Properties, 15 Different Standards — A Procurement Nightmare

A U.S.-based hotel management company operating 15 mid-scale to upper-midscale properties faced a growing procurement crisis. Each property had been sourcing FF&E independently — different vendors, different specifications, inconsistent quality.

  • Inconsistent guest experience — guests noticed furniture quality varied between properties, undermining brand confidence.
  • Duplicated procurement effort — 15 separate purchasing processes, 15 vendor negotiations, 15 QC programs — each property managed its own sourcing.
  • Brand audit failures — 3 of 15 properties failed brand standard inspections due to non-compliant furniture finishes and fire ratings.
  • Higher total costs — no volume leverage, inconsistent specs led to rework, and management time was spread thin across 15 vendor relationships.
Technical Solution

One Specification, One Supplier, 15 Identical Outcomes

foshanFFE served as the single-source FF&E integrator, creating unified standards and deploying them across all 15 properties in a phased rollout:

Unified Specification Manual

Complete FF&E spec package covering casegoods, soft seating, lighting, drapery, mattresses, and bath vanities — with material standards, finish samples, and fire ratings documented for every item.

Brand Standard Compliance

All items certified to CARB P2, TB 117-2013, UL listed (lighting), and ADA-compliant dimensions. Intertek and SGS tested per brand audit checklist.

Prototype Room Approval

Full prototype guestroom built in Foshan factory, reviewed by brand's design team via video inspection. Approved as the master reference for all 15 properties.

Phased Rollout Plan

15 properties deployed in 4 waves (4+4+4+3) over 6 months. Each wave used identical specs with staggered production and shipping to match property renovation schedules.

Frequently Asked Questions

We start by obtaining the brand's complete FF&E specification manual — material standards, finish samples, fire ratings, and dimensional requirements. A prototype room is built and approved by the brand's design team before mass production begins. Every unit across all properties is then produced to the identical approved sample, with QC inspections at each production stage.
North American hotel FF&E must meet multiple standards: CARB P2 (formaldehyde emission for composite wood), TB 117-2013 (upholstered furniture flammability), BS 7176 or CAL TB 133 (contract-grade upholstery fire rating), UL listing (electrical items like lamps), and ADA compliance (accessible room furniture dimensions). All products in this project met or exceeded these requirements, verified by SGS and Intertek testing.
Centralized sourcing consolidates volume across all properties into a single procurement program. This creates economies of scale in material purchasing, reduces per-unit shipping costs, eliminates duplicated management effort, and standardizes quality. In this project, centralized procurement delivered 28% cost savings and 40% management overhead reduction.
