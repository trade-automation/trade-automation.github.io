Project Background

A London-based restaurant group opened a new boutique café-restaurant concept in a converted Victorian building in central London. The space — 200m² across ground floor and mezzanine — required a complete FF&E package spanning 15 distinct product categories. Rather than coordinating 8–10 separate suppliers (furniture, lighting, tiles, cladding, millwork, signage...), the owner chose a single integrator to manage the entire scope on one purchase order.

The design called for a warm industrial aesthetic: exposed steel frames, emerald green velvet booth seating, walnut dining tables, a curved GFRC (glass-fibre reinforced concrete) bar counter, and pendant lighting with brass finishes. Every element had to work together as a cohesive visual language while meeting strict UK fire safety regulations for commercial dining spaces.

Two constraints shaped the project: an 8-week timeline from design approval to container loading (driven by the building's construction schedule), and London's nighttime delivery restrictions for the installation phase. Central London's congestion zone limits commercial vehicle access to specific hours, requiring precise coordination between shipping schedule and the site's installation crew.

Challenges & Solutions

Challenge 1

15 Categories from One Source in 8 Weeks

The restaurant group was accustomed to managing multiple specialist suppliers. Consolidating 15 product categories — spanning furniture, metalwork, concrete, lighting, tiles, and millwork — into a single PO with one delivery date was a significant coordination challenge.

Solution

Activated the 19-partner Foshan production network. Furniture produced in-house, GFRC outsourced to a specialist concrete fabricator, brass lighting sourced from a dedicated metal workshop, porcelain tiles from a Guangdong ceramic factory. All items converged at the consolidation warehouse, packed into 2 containers on a synchronized 8-week schedule. Zero items missed the loading window.

Challenge 2

Custom Curved Booth Seating — Design Fidelity

The designer specified sweeping S-curve booth seats with precise radii (1800mm arc, R600mm inner curve). Achieving this geometry in plywood shell construction while maintaining Crib 5 fire compliance required careful prototyping.

Solution

Built 2 full-scale booth prototypes in week 1, shipped air-freight to London for designer review. Approved with one minor radius adjustment (R600→R550mm). Final production used CNC-cut plywood shells with FR-treated foam and velvet upholstery. All 6 units passed BS 5852 Crib 5 on first test.

Challenge 3

London Nighttime Delivery & Installation Constraints

Central London's congestion zone restricts commercial vehicle access. The installation crew could only receive deliveries between 20:00–06:00, and the building's neighbors enforced strict noise limits after 22:00.

Solution

Pre-labeled every crate with room-zone code and installation sequence number. Provided the London crew with a detailed floor-plan showing exact placement positions. Container arrival was timed for 19:30, allowing the crew to begin unloading at the 20:00 window. All installation completed within 3 night shifts — no noise complaints, no delays.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you furnish a complete restaurant with furniture, tiles, lighting, and millwork on one PO?

Yes. The 19-partner Foshan production network covers furniture, metalwork, concrete, lighting, ceramics, and millwork. All categories consolidated at one warehouse and shipped in one or two containers — single PO, single delivery.

Can you produce custom-shaped booth seating?

Yes. Full-scale prototypes are built using CNC-cut plywood shells to match any curve or radius. Prototypes can be air-freighted for client review before mass production. Fire compliance (BS 5852, EN 1021) is maintained across all custom shapes.

What is the minimum project size handled?

Projects from $50K to $3M FOB are regularly handled. Even smaller projects like a 200m² café benefit from the consolidation model — 15 categories on one PO means one quality standard, one delivery schedule, and one point of accountability.
