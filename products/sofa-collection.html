Hotel Sofa Collection

Contract sofas for guestrooms, suites, lobbies and public areas. Modular, lounge, sculptural, straight and corner configurations, built on hardwood frames with hospitality foam, commercial fabric and fire certification to British or US practice.

Product Overview

A hotel sofa is asked to do something a residential sofa never is. It has to survive a decade of guests who sit on it in wet swimwear, drag a suitcase past it and put their feet on the arm. It has to pass a fire test on the finished assembly, not only on the foam. And it has to get into the room in the first place, through a 900 mm door, around a corridor corner and up a lift that was sized before anyone chose the furniture.

This collection covers the five sofa types that appear in a hotel FF&E package: modular sofas for lobbies and lounge areas, lounge and accent sofas for suites and executive floors, sculptural sofas for branded public spaces, straight sofas as the guestroom and suite workhorse, and corner sofas for suites and residence keys. Every model is available in custom length, depth and seat height, with fabric, leather or COM material handling.

What is shown below is a working range, not a fixed catalogue. Dimensions, foam density, fabric and fire specification are set per project. Where a model is close but not exact, the frame is redrawn rather than substituted.

What Makes a Contract Sofa Different From a Residential One

Four things separate a sofa that lasts eight years in a hotel from one that lasts three.

Frame. Residential frames are often softwood or particle board with stapled joints. Contract frames are kiln-dried hardwood, corner blocks glued and screwed, with 18 mm plywood at the load points where the arms meet the seat. The difference shows up at year five, when a stapled frame has started to rack and the sofa no longer sits flat.

Foam. Hotel seating foam is specified by density, not by feel. A guestroom occasional chair can use 30 to 35 kg/m3; a lobby sofa needs 35 to 40 kg/m3, and a bar or restaurant sofa 40 kg/m3 or above. Below 30 kg/m3 the seat takes a permanent set within a year of regular use, and no amount of re-covering fixes it.

Fabric. This is where most of the cost difference sits, and where most projects get it wrong. A furnishing-weight velvet at 15,000 double rubs is fine in a bedroom used twelve nights a year. In a hotel it abrades through at the seat front within two years. Contract fabric starts at 25,000 rubs for guestroom occasional seating and runs to 50,000 or 100,000 for public areas.

Fire performance. Residential furniture is tested as components. Contract furniture is tested as an assembly, meaning fabric, foam, interliner and frame together, to BS 5852 ignition source 5, or BS 7176 by hazard level, or CAL TB 133 in US public areas. A sofa that passes on the foam alone can still fail as an assembly if the fabric and interliner were chosen without reference to each other.

How a Sofa Gets Into a Guestroom

This question decides a specification more often than design does, and it is usually left until after the PO. Four dimensions govern it.

  • Guestroom door clear width. Typically 800 to 900 mm with the door open at 90 degrees and the stop in place. Measure the clear opening, not the frame.
  • Corridor corner. A 2.4 metre sofa will not turn a 90-degree corner into a 1.2 metre wide corridor, whatever fits through the door.
  • Lift car. Internal width, depth and door height. A 410 cm sofa goes up a service lift in five pieces; it does not go up in one.
  • Room geometry. A chaise end can block the window reveal or the wardrobe door. Worth drawing the sofa into the room plan before it is ordered.

Three build options solve most access problems, and all three are quoted at the same stage as the sofa rather than as an afterthought:

  • Modular construction. Seat units of 67 to 140 cm that bolt together on site. The default for anything over 2 metres.
  • Removable back. The back panel unbolts and re-fits with the same fixings. Adds roughly 3 to 5 days to production and nothing to the price.
  • Detachable legs. Standard on most models. Relevant where the door clear width is under 850 mm.

Send the door width, corridor corner and lift dimensions with the enquiry and the frame is drawn to suit before quotation, not after the sample is approved.

Minimum Order Quantity and Lead Time

Minimum order is 10 pieces per model, and mixed containers are accepted. A project can combine sofas with casegoods, seating and lighting in one shipment, which is how most of our orders actually ship.

Production runs 35 to 45 days after deposit and fabric approval. Repeat models on an approved fabric run in 25 days. For a full guestroom package the cycle is longer, 30 days for the sample and 60 days for bulk production, because the critical path moves to the casegoods and millwork rather than the upholstery.

Fabric approval is the most common cause of delay. If the fabric is being sourced or a strike-off is being approved by the designer, that clock starts before ours does, and it is worth building four to six weeks into the programme for it.

Quality Standards and Certifications

  • BS 5852 Crib 5 - ignition source 5 testing on the finished sofa assembly, for UK and Middle East public-area specifications.
  • BS 7176 - Low, Medium and High Hazard seating classification by end use.
  • BS 7177 - Medium Hazard for mattresses and bed bases in guestrooms, where a sofa and bed base are supplied together.
  • CAL TB 117-2013 - US foam flammability, required on all US hotel projects.
  • CAL TB 133 - US public-area seating, the stricter of the two American standards.
  • CARB Phase 2 / TSCA Title VI - formaldehyde limits on any plywood or MDF in the frame or integrated cabinet.
  • EUDR - timber due diligence documentation for EU-bound shipments.
  • ISO 9001 - factory quality management, with in-line inspection and a pre-shipment report per order.

Test reports are supplied with the quotation where a standard report already exists, and commissioned on the finished assembly where the project specifies a test we have not run. Expect three to four weeks for a commissioned assembly test.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size sofa fits a standard hotel guestroom?

Most guestrooms take a 1.4 to 1.8 metre two-seat sofa, or a 1.2 metre armchair-and-a-half where the room is tight. The constraint is rarely floor area, it is the walkway. A sofa should leave 600 mm of clear circulation in front of it and 900 mm at the foot of the bed. In rooms under 28 square metres a 1.5 metre two-seat sofa against the window wall usually works better than a three-seat.

Can a hotel sofa be built to fit through a guestroom door?

Yes, and it should be specified that way from the start. Standard guestroom door clear width is 800 to 900 mm. Any sofa over 2 metres should either split into seat units or have a removable back and detachable legs. Modular sofas solve this by design, since the units are 67 to 140 cm wide. On fixed sofas the back panel and legs come off at the factory and are re-fitted on site. Give us the door width, corridor corner and lift dimensions at quotation stage and the frame is drawn to suit.

What Martindale rating should hotel sofa fabric have?

25,000 double rubs for guestroom occasional seating, 40,000 for suite and lounge sofas, and 50,000 to 100,000 for lobby, bar and restaurant seating. Below 25,000 the fabric shows pile crush and abrasion within two years in a hotel. Above 100,000 you are usually paying for a technical weave you do not need in a bedroom. Test reports are available for every fabric we quote.

What fire standard applies to hotel sofas?

In UK and Middle East projects specified to British practice, the assembly is tested to BS 5852 with ignition source 5 (Crib 5) for public areas, and BS 7176 Low or Medium Hazard for guestrooms. In the United States, foam is tested to CAL TB 117-2013 and public-area seating to CAL TB 133. Chinese contract factories build to either route. The test is done on the finished assembly rather than on components alone, so fabric, foam and interliner have to be selected together.
