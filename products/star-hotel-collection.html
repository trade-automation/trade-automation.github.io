Product Overview

Hotel star ratings set expectations that extend to every piece of furniture a guest touches, sees, and experiences. The 4-star hotel fit-out approach prioritizes durability, ease of maintenance, and cost efficiency without sacrificing the guest experience.

Where the Five-Star Detail Is Made

A headboard is the one piece of guestroom upholstery a guest looks at from a pillow, in daylight, at close range. It is also the piece most often delivered wrong, because it was built to a standard size instead of to the room.

Upholstered headboards are quoted by the room here, not by the piece. Width, height, the reveal above the mattress and the position of any inset are set from the guestroom drawing, because a headboard sitting 40mm proud of the bed reads as an error from the doorway and cannot be corrected on site.

The bouclé field and the striped inset in the photograph are matched before the cover goes on, and the inset fabric is reserved from the same roll as the field so the two do not separate into different whites under warm guestroom lighting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How do I budget FF&E for a new hotel project?

A: Typical FF&E budget allocation: 40-50% guestroom furniture, 20-25% public areas (lobby, restaurant, bar), 10-15% outdoor/pool, 10-15% back-of-house, 5-10% contingency. foshanFFE.com provides detailed cost breakdowns per room and per public area based on your floor plans and target star rating. Contact us for a free preliminary budget estimate.

Q: What is the cost difference between 4-star and 5-star hotel furniture?

A: The jump from 4-star to 5-star typically adds 50-80% to per-room FF&E cost, driven by: natural wood veneer vs. HPL finishes, solid wood vs. engineered cores, premium hardware (soft-close, concealed hinges), higher-density foam (35kg/m³ vs. 25kg/m³), and custom vs. semi-custom designs. foshanFFE.com can propose value-engineered alternatives that achieve 5-star aesthetics at 4-star budgets.
