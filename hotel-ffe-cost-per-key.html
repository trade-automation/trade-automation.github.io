The Short Answer

What FF&E Costs per Key

FOB Foshan FF&E pricing runs $2,500–$4,500 per key for 3-star and select-service hotels, $4,500–$8,000 per key for 4-star upscale properties, and $9,000–$16,000 per key for 5-star luxury projects. Casegoods alone run $1,800–$3,200 per key. These figures exclude ocean freight, duty and installation.

Per key means per guestroom. The public areas, food and beverage outlets, meeting space and back of house are allocated across the guestroom count, which is why a 120-key boutique and a 400-key resort at the same star rating can sit at different points in the same band.

Read the range as a planning tool, not a quotation. A per-key budget is what the owner needs to size the capital line. A per-key price is what a factory quotes once the drawing set is frozen.

Cost Drivers

What Pushes the Number Up

Two projects at the same star rating can differ by 40% on a per-key basis. These are the six variables that decide which end of the band a project lands on.

▢

Custom millwork

Reception desks, bar fronts, back bars and feature joinery are built to drawing, not to a catalogue. Millwork is the single largest line in a luxury package and the hardest to value-engineer without changing how the space reads.

▢

Upholstery grade and foam build

Contract-grade fabric, FR-treated foam and a build tested to CAL 117, BS 7177 or EN 1021 cost more than a residential equivalent. The difference shows up in year three, when a lobby chair is still holding its shape.

▢

Compliance testing

Test reports are per fabric and per foam build, not per supplier. A package with many fabrics across many categories carries more testing cost than one built on a tighter palette.

▢

Lighting and controls

Decorative fixtures are a design decision. Control gear is a specification decision: dimmable drivers, DALI-2 control and motorised drapery all add cost per room, and they add it to every room.

▢

Scope depth

A guestroom-only package sits at the low end of a tier. Adding lobby, restaurant, meeting, wellness and outdoor scope pulls the per-key figure up, because the public areas are allocated across the same key count.

▢

Landed cost

Freight, duty and installation sit outside the FOB figure. How the package is consolidated decides how much of it the owner pays: one booking on a single purchase order costs less per room than four partial shipments.

New Build vs Replacement

New Build and FF&E Replacement Cost per Key

Replacement cycles are priced on a different basis from new build, even when the room looks the same on paper. Four differences decide the number.

Dimension New build Replacement / renovation
Scope Every item in every area, from guestroom to back of house A defined scope, often guestrooms plus soft goods, with public areas phased or excluded
Reuse Nothing is reused Casegoods carcasses, mirrors and some lighting can be retained where they are sound
Cost position Higher per key, because public areas are inside the package Lower per key on a narrower scope, with the saving concentrated in the items not replaced
Procurement route One purchase order for the whole package, loaded in a single window Often phased floor by floor while the hotel trades, which adds handling and storage cost
Compliance Full documentation set for every category Documentation still required for any new upholstery, drapery or mattress
Lead time 10–14 weeks for a full package before ocean transit Shorter when the scope is narrower, because the finish schedule is already fixed and sampling is reduced

A replacement programme that keeps the casegoods but replaces the upholstery, drapery and mattresses is not simply a smaller new build. The retained items set the finish standard for the new ones, so matching the existing veneer and laminate is part of the scope and part of the cost.

Landed Cost

From FOB to Landed Cost per Key

Every figure on this page is FOB Foshan. The owner's budget line is the landed cost, and four additions sit between the two.

  • Ocean freight and inland haulage. Charged by volume, so the packing method and container loading plan decide the number as much as the furniture does.
  • Duty and tariff classification. Depends on the destination market and on how the package is classified and documented. Ask for a landed-cost estimate rather than assuming FOB plus freight.
  • Installation and site handling. Labour, lift access, offloading and floor protection. On a phased renovation these costs rise, because the property is trading while the work happens.
  • Storage and staging. Where the site cannot take the full delivery in one window, the package has to sit somewhere, and that is a cost line.

Consolidation is the one lever that reduces all four. A 300-room package covering casegoods, soft seating, dining furniture, lobby pieces and bath vanities consolidates into 15–25 containers loaded within a five-day window on a single booking. Splitting the same scope across separate suppliers produces multiple partial containers, multiple freight bookings and multiple sets of export documents.

Reading a Quote

How to Compare Two per-Key Prices

A per-key price is only comparable when both quotes cover the same scope on the same terms. Four questions settle it.

  • Which categories are inside the number? Casegoods only, or casegoods plus seating, lighting, drapery, mattresses and vanities.
  • Are public areas allocated? A per-key figure that includes lobby and restaurant scope is not comparable with a guestroom-only figure.
  • What is excluded? Packing, inland haulage, documentation, freight, duty, installation and spares are the usual exclusions.
  • What is the compliance position? Test reports per fabric and foam build, or a supplier declaration.

Foshan pricing runs 30–40% below comparable Vietnamese or Indonesian output for the same specification, which is what makes the upper end of each band affordable rather than a reason to drop the specification. Standard production runs 6–8 weeks, and specialty finishes run 14–18 weeks.
