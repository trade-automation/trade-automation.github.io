The Short Answer

FF&E Demand Index: 98.2 for October 2026

The foshanFFE FF&E Demand Index reads 98.2 in its October 2026 edition, where 100 equals the same period one year earlier. That is down 1.8%, a softening reading. The drag comes from the two heaviest components: US lodging construction spending is down 10.0% year on year and global hotel rooms under construction are down 4.0%. Europe is moving the other way, with hotel rooms under construction up 3.3% and EU construction output up 1.6%.

This is a foshanFFE-built composite, not an official statistic. Every input value below is published data and every input is linked to its source. The weighting, the winsorisation and the arithmetic are ours, and they are printed in full in the methodology section so the reading can be reproduced or challenged.

One edition is a snapshot, not a trend. Version 1 of this index publishes a single reading. A time series requires historical pipeline and trade data on a consistent quarterly grid, which is on the roadmap for version 2.

Scope

What the FF&E Demand Index Measures

Hotel FF&E orders are placed in a narrow window. Casegoods, seating, lighting, mattresses and drapery are specified roughly 3 to 9 months before a property opens, and refurbishment FF&E follows the operator's ability to fund it out of cash flow. The index therefore tracks four things that sit upstream of an FF&E purchase order:

1

Construction capital actually being spent

The U.S. Census Bureau's lodging construction spending series measures dollars put in place on hotel projects each month. It is the closest published proxy for FF&E-relevant capex in the largest single buying market.

2

Rooms physically under construction

Rooms under construction are the near-term FF&E order book. Lodging Econometrics separates this stage from early planning, which is why the index uses rooms under construction rather than the headline pipeline count.

3

Construction sector output in Europe

The Eurostat construction production index tracks whether European builders are busier or quieter than a year earlier. It captures fit-out activity that never appears in a hotel pipeline report, including conversions and partial renovations.

4

Physical FF&E volume leaving China

UN Comtrade records the tonnage of seats, furniture, mattresses and lighting exported from China. It is the only component in the index that counts physical product rather than contracts or capital.

The index is expressed against a rolling base of 100, where 100 is the same month or quarter one year earlier. A reading above 100 means the demand backdrop is stronger than a year ago. A reading below 100 means it is weaker.

Where the Reading Comes From

Component Contributions to the 98.2 Reading

Each component pulls the composite up or down by its weight multiplied by its year-over-year change. The two US and global construction components more than offset the three positive ones, which is why the composite lands below 100.

Net effect: -1.85 percentage points, giving a reading of 98.2 against a base of 100.

C2. Global Hotel Rooms Under Construction

Latest value: 1,043,290 rooms across 6,174 projects in the second quarter of 2026, against 1,086,245 rooms across 6,257 projects in the second quarter of 2025. That is -3.95% in rooms and -1.33% in projects.

Definition: rooms in the construction stage of Lodging Econometrics' Hotel Construction Pipeline Trend Report, which tracks projects from early planning through to opening. Rooms under construction are the stage that maps most closely onto near-term FF&E procurement.

Context: the wider global pipeline, counting all stages, reached 15,976 projects and 2,433,948 rooms in the second quarter of 2026, against 15,871 projects and 2,436,225 rooms a year earlier. The renovation and brand conversion segment moved to 3,760 projects and 521,849 rooms, up 8% by projects and 5% by rooms year on year.

Source: Lodging Econometrics, global hotel construction pipeline, Q2 2026 and the Q2 2025 comparison release.

C3. Europe Hotel Rooms Under Construction

Latest value: 116,172 rooms across 773 projects in the second quarter of 2026, against 112,483 rooms across 744 projects a year earlier. That is +3.28% in rooms and +3.90% in projects.

Definition: the same construction-stage measure applied to the European region. The wider European pipeline stands at 1,736 projects and 255,976 rooms, up from 1,690 projects and 248,972 rooms.

Germany: 146 projects and 24,821 rooms in the second quarter of 2026, down from 157 projects and 26,861 rooms a year earlier. Germany is the only large European market in the report that contracted over the year.

Source: Lodging Econometrics, Europe hotel construction pipeline, Q2 2026 and the Q2 2025 comparison release.

C4. EU Construction Production Index

Latest value: 105.5 in August 2026 against 103.8 in August 2025, on an index where 2021 equals 100 and the series is seasonally and calendar adjusted. That is +1.64% year on year.

Definition: Eurostat's monthly production in construction index for the European Union of 27 countries, covering construction of buildings and civil engineering. It is a volume measure of building activity, so it picks up fit-out and refurbishment work that a hotel pipeline report does not capture.

Source: Eurostat, production in construction, monthly data (sts_copr_m), dataset DOI 10.2908/STS_COPR_M.

C5. China FF&E Export Volume

Latest value: 27.75 million tonnes of FF&E-relevant furniture exported from China in 2025, against 26.45 million tonnes in 2024. That is +4.92% in volume. The same four HS codes were worth $112.10 billion in 2025 against $121.72 billion in 2024, a fall of 7.91%.

Definition: the combined net weight of Chinese exports under HS 9401 (seats), HS 9403 (other furniture and parts), HS 9404 (mattresses and bedding) and HS 9405 (lamps and lighting). Volume is used for the index because it strips out price movement and exchange rate effects, leaving the physical quantity of FF&E product moving into projects worldwide.

The divergence matters. Volume rose while dollar value fell, which is consistent with unit prices falling rather than orders shrinking. For a buyer, that points to a market where suppliers are competing on price and where the same FF&E scope costs less in nominal dollars than it did a year earlier.

Caveat: this component is annual. China has not submitted monthly customs records to Comtrade's public tier, and Comtrade flags these records as aggregate estimates rather than reporter submissions. The component therefore carries the lowest weight of the five and is always shown against the same annual basis.

Source: UN Comtrade, reporter China (156), partner World, annual, flow exports.

C6. foshanFFE Own-Demand Signal (proprietary, weight 0 in version 1)

Latest value: 858 organic search impressions for foshanffe.com in the 14 days from 2026-10-05 to 2026-10-18, against 163 in the equivalent window one month earlier, with 8 clicks in the latest window.

Definition: Google Search Console impressions for this domain, which show how many times the site appeared in search results. It is a forward-looking indicator of inbound procurement interest, but it measures one company's visibility rather than the market, so it is published as a monitoring signal.

Why it carries zero weight in version 1: the property has roughly seven weeks of Search Console history, so there is no year-over-year comparison. A month-over-month window on a site in its launch phase is not a market signal. The component will be given a weight once twelve months of history exist.

Methodology

How the Index Is Built

The formula

Index = 100 + sum of (weight × winsorised year-over-year change) for each component.

Each component's year-over-year change is expressed in percentage points and winsorised at plus or minus 15 percentage points. Winsorisation caps an extreme input so that one volatile series cannot dictate the reading. In the October 2026 edition no component reached the cap, so the winsorised values equal the raw values.

The weights are a design decision, not an econometric result. They are published so that a reader who disagrees with them can recalculate. The component values and the arithmetic are both on this page.

Base and comparison period

Base is 100, defined as the same month or quarter one year before the component's latest reading. There is no fixed base year in version 1 because there is only one edition. When a historical series is published, the base will move to a fixed reference year and the back series will be restated.

Reference periods in this edition

Monthly components (C1 and C4) use August 2026 against August 2025. Quarterly components (C2 and C3) use the second quarter of 2026 against the second quarter of 2025. The annual component (C5) uses 2025 against 2024. The proprietary component (C6) uses a 14-day window against the equivalent window one month earlier and carries no weight.

Reproducing the reading

The whole edition is produced by a single Python script that pulls each source, prints the raw values and writes them to JSON. Re-running the script regenerates the component values used on this page. The component JSON for this edition is available on request from info@foshanFFE.com.

Limitations

  1. One edition exists. There is no historical index series yet, so the reading cannot be compared with prior editions.
  2. The inputs are published at three different frequencies and are not perfectly synchronised. The August 2026 monthly inputs and the second-quarter 2026 quarterly inputs describe overlapping but not identical periods.
  3. Europe sits inside the global series. C2 already contains the European rooms counted in C3. The overlap is deliberate, because Europe is weighted separately as a target market, and it means C2 and C3 should not be read as independent evidence.
  4. The trade component is annual and flagged by Comtrade as an aggregate estimate rather than a reporter submission.
  5. The index measures direction and volume, not price. It cannot be used to forecast cost per key or to price a package.
  6. Google Trends was evaluated as a component and excluded. No reliable programmatic access was available at the time of publication, so no Google Trends figure appears anywhere on this page.
  7. Site analytics were evaluated as a component and excluded. The proprietary signal that is published, C6, is organic search impressions only.
  8. Winsorisation at plus or minus 15 percentage points is a judgement call. It did not bind in this edition.

What It Means

What a 98.2 Reading Means for Hotel Developers and Procurement Teams

A reading below 100 describes a demand backdrop that is softer than a year earlier. It is not a forecast, and it is not a reason to delay a project. It does change the balance of the conversation with suppliers.

↓

US new-build buyers hold the better hand

Lodging construction spending is down 10.01% year on year and global rooms under construction are down 3.95%. Factory capacity that was committed to hotel programmes is looking for work. That is the moment to ask for firm pricing, extended payment terms or a shorter production window.

↑

European buyers face a tighter market

European rooms under construction rose 3.28% and EU construction output rose 1.64%. Fit-out capacity in Europe is busier. Buyers with European schedules should lock production slots earlier than the US comparison would suggest.

⇄

Renovation demand is holding up

The renovation and brand conversion segment of the global pipeline grew 8% in project terms year on year, even as new-build construction fell. Refurbishment scopes are usually smaller and faster, which suits a phased FF&E order better than a single large new-build package.

−

Volume is up while value is down

Chinese FF&E export volume rose 4.92% while the dollar value of the same shipments fell. More product is moving for less money. Buyers who can commit volume should test that against a live quotation rather than assuming last year's cost per key still holds.

Two practical notes. First, a single edition is not a trend; the reading should be read alongside the component values, not instead of them. Second, the index is a market backdrop, not a procurement plan. The specification, room count, compliance regime and delivery date of a specific project still determine what that project costs.
