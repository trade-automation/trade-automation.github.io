Hotel FF&E Checklist

The Hotel FF&E Procurement Checklist
Eight Stages, Start to Warranty

A stage-by-stage checklist for hotel developers, owners and procurement teams, written to be worked through rather than read

Start at Stage 1

The Short Answer

What This Checklist Covers

A hotel FF&E checklist runs from design freeze to warranty closeout: drawing set and quantities, specification and compliance standards, tender and price comparison, sampling and mockup approval, production and mid-production inspection, pre-shipment inspection, delivery and installation, then punch list and warranty handover. Each row below names the item to check and what finished work looks like.

The stages follow the order money is actually committed. Buyers who start at stage four, with a drawing set that is not frozen, end up paying for changes during production, which is the most expensive place to make them.

Print it, or copy the rows into the project schedule and assign an owner to each. The sign-off column is the part that matters.

Categories to Specify, by Area

Area Categories Watch Out For
Guestroom Casegoods, beds and headboards, seating, tables, lighting, drapery, bedding, in-room equipment, art Brand standard dimensions and finish codes
Bathroom Vanity units, countertops, mirrors, mirror lighting, shower screens, bath hardware Sanitaryware often sits with the base building
Corridor and lift lobby Console tables, benches, seating, decorative lighting, art, planters Footprint limits in a 1.5–2m corridor
Lobby and public areas Reception millwork, seating, tables, feature lighting, rugs, art Contract-grade construction for the abuse level
F&B Dining tables and chairs, banquettes, bar stools, bar millwork, buffet counters, terrace sets Kitchen equipment is a separate package
Meeting and banquet Banquet chairs and tables, conference tables, pre-function seating, staging Storage plan for stacked chairs
Wellness and spa Fitness equipment, treatment tables, changing room lockers and benches, pool loungers Fitness equipment is sometimes bought direct from the brand

Back of house Office furniture, staff canteen, lockers, linen room shelving, security desks Frequently omitted, then reordered at a higher unit price

Compliance Check

Standards to Confirm Before Production

Which standards apply depends on where the hotel is built. The documents to collect are the same in every case: a test report or certificate per material build, held against the purchase order.

Standard Applies To Document to Collect
CAL 117 Upholstered furniture sold in California Test report per fabric and foam build
CAL TB 133 Upholstered seating in public occupancies Full-scale test report
BS 7177 Upholstered furniture for UK and GCC projects Test report and label
BS 5867 Curtains and drapes Fabric test report
EN 1021 Upholstered furniture in the EU Ignition source test report
EN 13501 Reaction to fire classification for building products Classification report
NFPA 701 Drapery and textiles Test report
NFPA 262 Plenum-rated cable and some fixtures Test report
ISO 9001 Factory quality management system Current certificate
FSC Timber and wood products Chain of custody certificate
GREENGUARD Low-emission furniture and materials Certification record

Scheduling and PO Structure

Delivery and Purchase Order Reminders

  • One purchase order for the whole package. It is what allows the loading window to be planned as a single booking instead of four partial shipments.
  • Container count follows the scope. A 300-room package covering casegoods, soft seating, dining furniture, lobby pieces and bath vanities consolidates into 15–25 containers loaded within five days.
  • Lead times are 6–8 weeks for standard production and 10–14 weeks for a full package before ocean transit. Speciality finishes run 14–18 weeks.
  • Lock the contract four to five months before the target opening date. That covers production, transit and installation with room for one recovery cycle.
  • Decide who carries duty, installation and site storage before the price is finalised. Those three lines are the usual source of a late budget gap.
  • Keep back-of-house furniture in the same order. A second order for office and housekeeping furniture means a second freight booking and a higher unit price.

The destination market sets which standards apply. US projects usually call for CAL 117 or CAL TB 133 on upholstery. NFPA 701 applies to drapery and NFPA 262 to plenum-rated cable and some fixtures. Factory-level documentation usually includes ISO 9001, FSC and GREENGUARD.

The owner or project manager signs the commercial stages. The designer signs specification, sampling and mockup. The compliance lead signs fire and material documentation. Quality control signs first-article, mid-production and pre-shipment inspection. Finance signs payment milestones and the final release.
