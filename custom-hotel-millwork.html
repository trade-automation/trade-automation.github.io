Custom Hotel Millwork: Built From Approved Drawings, Not Intent

Millwork fails for one reason more often than any other: the thing that got built was not the thing that got approved. This page covers what counts as millwork, how the drawing chain is controlled, where tolerances are won or lost, and what we need in order to price it.

The short answer

Custom hotel millwork is the joinery built to the room rather than bought for it: reception desks, back bars, wardrobes, vanities, headboard walls, corridor and lift-lobby panelling, banquettes and fixed seating.

The value we add is not the saw. It is the drawing chain: one approved revision, one mockup, one inspection standard, one party answering when the fitted item does not meet the wall.

What Counts as Millwork on a Hotel Project

Seven categories cover most of a hotel package. Each has a different failure mode, so each is inspected differently.

Reception & concierge desks

Curved or faceted, cable management, transaction top, staff-side storage. Failure mode: substrate movement at the curve.

Back bar & F&B joinery

Glass display, undercounter refrigeration housings, wet-zone sealing. Failure mode: moisture ingress at the counter joint.

Wardrobes & closet systems

Hanging, shelving, safe housing, sliding or hinged fronts. Failure mode: door alignment after the first season.

Bathroom vanities

Wall-hung or floor-standing, stone top, plumbing cut-outs. Failure mode: unsealed core swelling at the wet edge.

Headboard walls & panelling

Upholstered or timber, integrated lighting, acoustic backing. Failure mode: fixings that cannot be re-tensioned.

Corridor & lift-lobby joinery

Room-number surrounds, niche shelving, protective skirting. Failure mode: impact damage at trolley height.

Banquettes & fixed seating

Framed, upholstered, built to the booth dimension. Failure mode: foam collapse at the front edge.

The Drawing Chain: Six Gates, One Approved Revision

Every gate has an owner and an output. When a millwork item arrives wrong, it is almost always because a gate was skipped rather than because a factory made a mistake.

Gate What happens Who signs Output that must exist
1. Design intent Designer issues the FF package, elevations and finish schedule Designer Drawing set with revision number
2. Shop drawing Factory drafts constructible details: sections, substrate, fixings, joints Factory + us Shop drawing set, RFI list
3. Review and approval Designer and brand review; comments closed; revision frozen Designer / brand Stamped approval naming the revision
4. Mockup One representative unit built and inspected in the finish Designer + us Mockup acceptance note, photo record
5. Production Fabrication against the frozen revision, in-process checks at fixed points Factory + our QC In-process records, material certificates
6. Fit and snag Site fit-up, scribe to the actual wall, snag list closed Installer + us Snag list, close-out photos

Where Tolerances Are Won or Lost

These eight lines are the ones we will not leave to a verbal agreement. Each is written into the shop drawing before fabrication.

Item What we hold Why
Panel thickness 18mm nominal for carcasses, 25mm for work surfaces Below this, spans sag
Wall straightness Scribe allowance of 10mm minimum at every wall-fixed item No site wall is square; a scribe detail absorbs it
Floor level Adjustable feet or plinth with 15mm levelling range Screeds are never flat
Door reveal 2–3mm consistent reveal, self-closing hardware above 1,200mm height Reveal drift is the first thing an auditor photographs
Edge sealing All six faces sealed on moisture-exposed items Unsealed core is a one-season failure
Fixings Through-bolted to blocking, anti-tip where free-standing Guest safety, not aesthetics
Fire and smoke Fire-rated door sets and intumescent where the compartment requires it A joinery package cannot break a fire strategy
Finish and sheen Agreed sheen level per surface, sample-approved Sheen mismatches read as defects in photos

The Four Reasons Millwork Does Not Fit

Drawings that assume a square room

The most common cause. It is not a factory problem and it is not solved by remaking the item.

Measurement taken after fabrication

On refurbishment, existing walls, floors and services must be measured before the shop drawing is frozen.

Services not coordinated

Sockets, data, drainage and lighting cut-outs land inside a panel unless they are drawn on the joinery elevation.

Installation sequence ignored

A wardrobe that cannot enter the room because the door casing went in first is a site planning failure.

Millwork Questions

What is the difference between custom millwork and loose furniture?

Loose furniture arrives finished and gets placed. Custom millwork is built to the room: it is drawn, approved, fabricated and often fitted on site. Reception desks, back bars, wardrobes and wall panelling are millwork. A nightstand is furniture, unless it is built into the wall.

Who is responsible for site measurements?

On a new build we work from the architectural drawings and flag every dimension that depends on a site condition. On a refurbishment we ask for site measurements before fabrication, or build in a tolerance allowance with a scribe detail. A millwork item that assumes a square wall is a rework order.

Can you produce a mockup before the full order?

Yes, and on anything above a few units we recommend it. A mockup room or a single representative unit lets the designer, the brand and the contractor agree on the detail before the production run.

What drawings do you need to quote millwork?

The FF package or joinery details, the room elevations, the finish schedule and the hardware spec. If only a design intent package exists, we can quote against it with the understanding that the shop drawing stage will move the number.
